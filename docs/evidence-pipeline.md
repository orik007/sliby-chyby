# Evidence pipeline

## Authority

This is the only rule for collecting, routing, and packaging evidence and for producing unpublished assessment proposals. What counts as attributable conduct and which score it supports is defined only in [scoring.md](scoring.md).

## Principle: official records only, verified before use

Evidence consists only of official records. A party's own website or publication, a press release, and the media are never evidence and are not collected.

A source is added only after all of these hold, and one source at a time:

1. its structure is checked against the publisher's official documentation and against downloaded real data;
2. its completeness is measured on real data (how many records resolve, link, and attribute, and how many do not);
3. its collector has tests built from the verified structure; and
4. every run reports the source's quality figures, and a failed structural check stops the affected data from being used in that run.

## Chamber data rules

These rules were verified against the official schema documentation (psp.cz pages k=1302, k=1303, k=1308) and the live open data.

- **Agenda item.** A vote names its session number and agenda-item number. The collector resolves the item from the **approved** agenda: the proposed and approved agendas share `id_schuze` and `id_bod` and differ only in `pozvanka`. Items of type 6 (individual answers to written interpellations) are ignored. The vote record carries the agenda-item title and the print it concerns.
- **Decisive vote.** A vote is decisive only when an official print-history step names that exact vote and records one of the Chamber's final decisions: `souhlas`, `schváleno`, `neschválen`, `neschválena`, `zamítnut`, `nepřijat`, `setrváno`, `pozm. přijat`. Procedural steps such as `vrácen`, `opakovat`, `přerušeno`, or `odročeno` are not decisive. The collector never infers a decisive vote from timing or order.
- **Party vote.** The club membership of each MP on the vote date decides the party. A party direction requires a strict majority of all club members; a manual vote or a vote voided by the official record is never attributed.
- **Proposals.** Print kind 1 (government bill), 3 (state budget), and 4 (international treaty) are government proposals. Print kind 2 with proposer type 2 or 3 is a member bill; its proposers come from `tisky.id_osoba` or, for several proposers, `predkladatel.unl`. Only the submission step (`předložen`) is a proposal.
- **Print text.** A print's page (`www.psp.cz/sqw/text/tiskt.sqw`) groups its files under labelled sections. The proposal text is the DOCX file of the section “Návrh zákona včetně důvodové zprávy” (or “Návrh, důvodová zpráva, obsah zákona”) for a bill or state budget, and of the section “Text v českém jazyce” or “Text” for a treaty or another print; when that section has no DOCX file, its Word 97-2003 (.doc) file, read with its quotation marks as written. A PDF is never read: its text loses the paragraphs and splits words at line ends, and a scanned PDF has no text. Other sections (the regulatory impact assessment, the consolidated text with marked changes, EU compatibility, annexes) are never read. A bill's text ends at the heading “Důvodová zpráva”; the explanatory memorandum is not part of any law change.
- **Later texts.** A print's history page (`www.psp.cz/sqw/historie.sqw`) links the “Text návrhu zákona schváleného Poslaneckou sněmovnou” (the text the Chamber approved) and, after a Senate or presidential return on which the Chamber insisted, the “Text návrhu zákona přijatého Parlamentem České republiky”; each is one DOCX file. These links are not in the open data and are read from the history page of every bill (print kinds 1, 2, and 3), by the text-page kind they point to (`v=PZ` approved, `v=SZ` passed). A bill that the Senate rejected has no approved-text page; its approved text is the sub-print that the history names as “Návrh zákona ve znění, ve kterém byl postoupen Senátu” (for example 163/2, “Zákon ze Senátu”), read like a print text. A state budget goes from the Chamber to the President, and psp.cz publishes no approved text of it. When the Senate returns a bill to the Chamber, its resolution is a sub-print of kind “Usnesení Senátu” (`tisky_za.unl`) whose “Text” DOCX rejects the bill or lists amendments; psp.cz publishes no single text of the bill with the Senate's amendments.
- **Law changes.** A bill's text (print kinds 1 and 2) is divided at its part headings (“ČÁST PRVNÍ”, “ČÁST DRUHÁ”, “ČÁST STO DVACÁTÁ PRVNÍ”, …; one or two upper- or lower-case ordinal words, after the hundredth preceded by “sto”). Each part is one **law change**: usually the amendment of one act, or repealing or transitional provisions. A text without part headings, a state budget, a treaty, and any other print is one law change. The act a part amends is the first act named in its first amending sentence: the first paragraph that names an act (“Zákon č. X/RRRR Sb., …”, “V § … zákona č. X/RRRR Sb., …”, “Příloha k zákonu č. …”) and states an amendment (“mění”, “doplňuje”, “vkládá”, “nahrazuje”, “zrušuje”, “zní:”). A part that only creates new provisions amends no act. A print that is one law change also takes the acts listed in its official title (“zákon č. X/RRRR Sb.” or “o změně zákona č. X/RRRR Sb.”, but not an act cited only inside another act's name or amendment history). A state budget carries the identifier `cz-print-kind-state-budget`. A print without a readable DOCX or DOC proposal text is one law change with only its official title, reported in the run; when psp.cz publishes a later text of it, the parts of that text are its law changes, and their proposed version is known only by the title.
- **Versions of a law change.** A law change has up to three versions: **proposed** (from the proposal text), **approved** (from the text the Chamber approved), and **passed** (from the text passed by Parliament). The later texts are split the same way, and each of their parts is matched to at most one earlier part: the one that amends the same act (or, when it amends no act, has the same title) and whose text is most alike, or, without such a part, the one whose text is at least 30 % alike in words and numbers (a retitled part). A proposal without parts that the debate divided into parts is matched to the part it contains most of; its other parts count as added during the debate. A part found only in a later text is a law change added during the debate; a part missing from a later text was removed by it. A later version whose text, apart from the part heading (or whole, when it has none), is the same as the version before it is stored as the same, so models never read a text twice.
- **Effective date.** A part titled “Účinnost” states when the bill takes effect, which applies to all of it, so it is not a law change. Its text, without the part heading and title, is stored in the original wording with every version of every law change of the same text (`effectiveCz`, empty when the text has none), also when that version's text is stored as the same as an earlier one: the date often changes during the debate while the rest of the text does not.
- **Conduct and version.** Each attributable conduct concerns one version of each law change of its print, read from the print-history step of the vote (its stage in `typ_stavu.unl` and its action); a vote is conduct on a print only when that print's own history names it as a decisive step. A submission concerns the proposed version. Approval of a bill (`souhlas`, in the third reading or in the first reading under § 90(2)) concerns the approved version. After the Senate returned the bill (stage “Sněmovna (Senát)”), insisting on it (`setrváno`) concerns the passed version, or the approved version, which is the same text, when psp.cz lists no passed text; a vote on the Senate's amendments (`pozm. přijat`, `nepřijat`) concerns the approved text changed by them, which is the passed version, or, when psp.cz lists no passed text, the approved version, marked; and each such vote brings the Senate resolution, rejecting the bill or listing amendments, into the package once. After the President returned the bill (stage “Sněmovna (Prezident)”), a vote concerns the passed version; when psp.cz lists no passed text it is marked, because the text sent to the President can include accepted Senate amendments. Every other decisive vote (a treaty, a resolution, a rejection) concerns the proposed version; a rejection in the third reading is marked, because that vote concerned the proposed text as amended in the second reading, which psp.cz does not publish as one text. When the version a conduct concerns is not published, the conduct concerns the latest earlier version and is marked so.
- **Every run reads the texts again.** Print texts are downloaded again in every run and not cached, so a later text appears as soon as psp.cz publishes it and no assumption about unchanged files is needed. psp.cz closes the connection after every answer and drops new connections above roughly 45 to 50 a minute (measured in run 64), so the download keeps at most two requests open, starts attempts at least 1.5 s apart (at most 40 a minute), and gives up connecting after 3 s before retrying. A failed download fails the run. A text that psp.cz lists without a DOCX or DOC file, or such a file that cannot be read as text, is treated as not published and reported, and so is a bill whose approval is a decisive vote but which has no approved text, which shows when the history page could not be read. The extracted text is normalized (only the text of the document, runs of white space as one space, no empty paragraphs), so an unchanged document gives the same text and the same model-cache fingerprint.
- **Consistency check.** If the session data and the print history share no agenda-item identifier, the collector records a warning and does not use agenda items in that run.

## Law-change context

An amending text often does not state the wording it changes ("V § 52 odst. 1 se věta poslední zrušuje."). Every model that reads a law change therefore also receives its **context**: the wording of each provision its amending instructions name, as in force when the party acted. Context comes from the Collection of Laws, not from the party or the Chamber; it is not evidence of conduct, is not an evidence item, and never changes attribution or what counts as conduct.

**Source.** The open data of e-Sbírka, the official electronic Collection of Laws, read through its SPARQL endpoint `https://opendata.eselpoint.gov.cz/sparql`, which the official open-data manual (`https://opendata.eselpoint.gov.cz/dokumentace/Prirucka_OpenData_e-Sbirka.htm`) offers for exactly built queries, with no registration. The web application's internal interface is never used. Verified against the manual and real data on 28 September 2026: for every version of all 403 law changes then in packages, a wording in force on the conduct date existed, and 708 direct queries all succeeded (median 0.72 s, max 2.9 s).

**Rules.** These were verified on the same data:

- **Instructions.** Each version of a law change that amends an act is read instruction by instruction, only outside quoted new text. The introduction of the act (`Zákon č. … se mění takto:`) and renumbering (`Dosavadní odstavce … se označují jako …`) are not instructions. An instruction names its targets in the act it amends, or in the act it names: a § (`V § 52 …`, `§ 7 zní:`) and a paragraph it changes in place (`odst. 1`, `odstavec 2`; when it inserts or appends a paragraph, letter, sentence, or words, the whole §); an article and its point, also of an amending act (`V čl. II bodu 5 zákona č. 182/2024 Sb.`); an annex (`V příloze č. 3`, the only annex `Příloha k zákonu`) or an item of it (`V položce 18`); a footnote (`Poznámka pod čarou č. 79`); and, for a newly inserted §, the § after which it is inserted.
- **Wording.** The wording used is the one in force on the date of the earliest conduct that concerns the version (its submission or vote), with `účinnost-znění-od` on or before that date and `účinnost-znění-do`, if any, on or after it; the promulgated wording (`0000-00-00`) is never used. A version without conduct gets no context.
- **Locating a provision.** A provision is found only by its ELI path in that wording (`…/par_52`, `…/par_52/odst_1`, `…/cl_2/bod_5`, `…/prilohy/priloha_3`, `…/pol_18`, `…/poznamkypodcarou/ppc_79`; the only, unnumbered annex is `priloha_0`), never by its citation label, which some nodes lack. The structure of a wording is read once, by one query for the paths of all its nodes; texts are then read for exactly the nodes of each target and their descendants, in document order. A provision inside another supplied provision of the same wording is supplied once, within it.
- **Statuses.** Every target gets exactly one status, which the models see with the context:

| Status | Meaning | Context supplied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `found` | The provision exists in the wording on the conduct date. | Its text, with that wording's date. |
| `later-wording` | It is missing on that date but exists in a later wording: an earlier amendment, already promulgated, had inserted it with a later effect. | Its text from the nearest later wording, marked with that wording's date. |
| `paragraph-not-found` | The § exists, the named paragraph does not. | The whole §. |
| `new-provision` | The instruction itself inserts the provision. | None; there is no earlier text. |
| `not-found` | Neither the wording on the date nor any later wording contains it. | None. |
| `not-recognised` | The instruction names no target the rules above recognise (for example a whole part, head, or division, a title, or a replacement of words in the whole act). | None; the instruction is quoted. |

  A law change that amends no act (a new act, transitional or repealing provisions, a treaty, a budget) gets no context and no status.
- **Requests.** Only direct queries are sent, one at a time, at least 2 s apart: the wordings of one act (restricted to wordings), the node paths below one wording, and the texts of listed nodes. A query never scans the nodes of a wording by prefix and never combines several starting nodes with a path of any length; such queries exceeded the endpoint's time limit. A node list is requested with a limit of 50,000 rows, and an answer that fills it, or an empty structure of an existing wording, is a technical failure. A failed request is retried after 30 s and again after 90 s. A request fails technically when it gets no answer, an HTTP error, an unreadable answer, an answer the endpoint marks as incomplete, or an answer after 30 s or more (the endpoint returns partial results near its limit of about 60 s). After the last retry a technical failure fails the run. A complete answer that the act, wording, or provision does not exist is not a failure: it only sets the status. Every request (time, purpose, duration, outcome, bytes) is written to `law-context/e-sbirka-requests.jsonl` in the run artifact.
- **Cache.** A wording of an act does not change, so its structure and the texts of its nodes, with their document order, are stored permanently in the audit state, one append-only file per act (`automation/law-context/<year>-<number>.jsonl`), and are never requested or checked again. The list of an act's wordings grows when an amendment is promulgated, so it is stored with the day it was read and used only for conduct before that day; for later conduct it is read again, at most once per run and act. A run requests only what its law changes need and the cache lacks; only a new pipeline generation starts from an empty cache.

## Daily workflow

The workflow runs at 09:37 Europe/Prague and keeps a manual trigger. The schedule stays paused until a manual `workflow_dispatch` run of the current pipeline has been verified end to end (see `.github/workflows/collect-evidence.yml`).

1. **Collect.** Every run collects every active source completely from its `bootstrapFrom` date, so each package is built from the whole record history and a new package never loses earlier conduct. There is no manual start date or source subset. If any source fails, the run fails and nothing is saved. `checkpoints.json` only records each source's last successful collection; it never limits what is collected.
2. **Filter.** Only a record that can be attributable conduct of a tracked party under [scoring.md](scoring.md) continues: a decisive vote with a club result, or the submission of a member bill or government proposal. Attribution depends only on the record itself, so no other record can ever affect a score, and none is tagged or fetched further.
3. **Split prints into law changes.** Each print those records concern is split into law changes, each with its versions (see *Chamber data rules*). A law change keeps only its versions up to the latest one that the print's conduct up to the cutoff concerns, with the acts those versions amend: a later text did not yet exist at the cutoff, and a change added only in a later text is left out. A print without a proposal text whose parts are known only from a later text is, before that text, one law change known by its title. From here on the unit is the **law change**, not the print: a law change is screened, routed, packaged, and assessed. The print only links a law change to the party's conduct on it, the votes on the print and its submission, each of which concerns one version.
4. **Screen and tag every law change.** Two independent screeners each read the full text of every version of every law change (a print-wide Senate resolution is not part of a law change and goes only into the package); none is skipped or dropped by rule. A law change is substantive, and is routed, when any of its versions is. Each screener returns:
   - whether the change is **substantive** or only **technical**, with a reason. A technical change changes nothing for anyone: renumbered references, a renamed authority, a legislative correction, or an adaptation to another act without new content. Any change of an amount, rate, deadline, right, obligation, the people covered, or who pays or receives is substantive, even a single number. In doubt, the change is substantive. A law change is substantive when at least one screener says so, or when a screener output is invalid;
   - the specific (`assessmentEligible`) topic tags from the taxonomy (`automation/topic-routing/taxonomy-v2.json`) it concerns, or `unknown`, with a confidence from 0 to 1.

   Each screener reads every version with its context (*Law-change context*).
5. **Route.** A substantive law change goes to promises in two ways, and to the promises of both. A promise holds its routing tags in `automation/topic-routing/promise-tags.json`: one or more specific tags, assigned once when the promise is catalogued and reviewed in its pull request, never by a model during a run. Statements hold no tags and are never routed, packaged, or assessed.
   - **Direct link.** An act the change amends, or the state budget print kind, is listed under a tag in `directLegalIdentifiers` of the taxonomy. The change goes to every promise with that tag.
   - **Tags.** Every specific tag that either screener gives with a confidence of at least 0.5. The change goes to every promise with that tag. An `unknown` from one screener does not block the other's tags, and a broad parent tag alone never routes a change.

   A change that reaches no promise, because it has no specific tag or no promise holds one, is reported as unrouted; that never means no progress. Models may return only existing tag identifiers or `unknown`. A taxonomy change is a reviewed change, and its content hash recomputes every dependent classification. A promise and a routed law change continue only when the promise's party has attributable conduct on the change's print; routing never establishes attribution or a score.
6. **Check relevance.** For each remaining promise and law change, two independent checks read the promise (its title, which names the commitment assessed, and its exact wording) and the full text of every version of the change with its context and say whether the change is relevant to that promise, with a reason. The pair is kept when at least one check says relevant, or when a check output is invalid; it is dropped only when both say it is not relevant. Every dropped pair and both reasons are in the routing report.
7. **Package.** Each promise with at least one kept law change gets one evidence package: every kept law change with the full text of each of its versions and their context, the party's attributable conduct on each of their prints (decisive votes and the submission), each naming the version it concerns, and, once per print, the Senate resolution when a vote in the package followed the Senate's return. A law change carries only the versions up to the latest one its conduct concerns: a later text adds nothing to judging that conduct and may not have existed at the package cutoff. Nothing is trimmed and no excerpt is selected. Every package is assessed, whatever its size, as long as the models can read it: their context window is 1,050,000 tokens, of which their answer can take up to 128,000, so a request can carry at most 922,000 input tokens (OpenAI model documentation, verified on 26 September 2026). A package whose assessor request, the instructions and the package together, is larger (estimated at 2.5 characters per token, below the fewest measured in run 56) cannot be sent and is reported for human review. The arbiter also reads both reviews, so its request is checked before it is called; when it is larger, the arbiter is not called and the disagreement goes to human review. A request above 272,000 input tokens costs twice as much for input and one and a half times as much for output, and the spend report counts it so (`automation/model-prices.json`).
8. **Persist.** Before any assessment call, the collection record (`checkpoints.json`), the model caches, the law-change context cache, and the packages are saved together to the dedicated unpublished audit pull request, and only when collection, splitting, context, screening, routing, relevance checks, packaging, and persistence all succeed in the same run. The queue keeps at most one current package per promise: a rebuilt package whose assessable content is unchanged keeps the current one, a promise that no longer gets a package has its current package withdrawn, and superseded and withdrawn versions stay auditable.
9. **Assess.** Each new or changed package, up to the per-run cap, goes independently to two assessors, who may cite only evidence IDs in that package. Each assessor first gives a verdict on **every** law change in the package: relevant or not relevant to the exact promise, with a reason. Each conduct is then judged against the version it concerns, read with its context ([scoring.md](scoring.md)). An output that omits a law change or gives it two verdicts is invalid. Only relevant changes may support the proposed score. When their valid assessments differ, or exactly one output is invalid, an arbiter selects a usable assessment or returns no conclusion. Two invalid outputs write a private failure audit and re-enter a later batch unless a newer package replaces them. The cap (`max_assessments`, default `0`) is set on each manual run; packages above it stay queued for a later run, and the run reports their number and size so the cost can be estimated first. A scheduled run assesses nothing until a cap is set for it.
10. **Report.** The proposals and a coverage audit are written to the audit pull request. The audit covers every promise and active source and reports the run's data-quality figures: records skipped as not attributable, prints with a proposal, approved, or passed text, law changes added or removed during the debate, approved bills without an approved text, law changes, technical and substantive changes, direct links, unrouted law changes, pairs dropped by the relevance check, packages larger than the models can read, invalid model outputs, and, for the context, the law changes that amend an act, the targets by status, and the e-Sbírka requests made and failed. The pull request text lists only what needs a person: every current proposal whose status differs from the promise's current public score (the latest revision in `data/assessment-revisions` on `main`), with its party, both statuses, how the proposal arose, and each assessor's and the arbiter's verdict with its reason; every current proposal left for human review; and every package larger than the models can read. Each proposal records the public revision it was compared with. It leaves the list when a later public revision is published, whatever score that revision gives, or when a newer proposal supersedes it. A listed proposal is marked when this run created it, when its package has changed since and no new proposal has been made yet, or when the promise no longer has a current package. New proposals that confirm the public score, unchanged packages, packages deferred by the cap, and failed assessments are only counted; the full list is in the run's `assessment-batch.json`. Model-written text is shown as plain text. When the list does not fit GitHub's size limit for the pull request text, it continues, in order and complete, in numbered comments on the pull request; each run edits its previous continuation comments in place, adds missing ones, and deletes extra ones, so they never pile up. A reason longer than 1,500 characters is cut in the list and kept whole on the proposal page, and at most 40 continuation comments are written; anything beyond them is counted and left to the proposal pages and `assessment-batch.json`. The text also counts the law changes the assessors found not relevant, by the tag or direct link that routed them, so an overly broad tag is visible.

**Run log.** Every step writes what it is doing to the GitHub Actions log: which source it downloads, how many items it processes, and progress in batches. Every paid model call writes one line with its model, processing tier, tokens, and estimated cost, and is appended to `model-calls.jsonl` in the run artifact. Every download attempt to psp.cz for print texts (its time, duration, HTTP status, or the transport error code) and every new connection opened to psp.cz (how long it took to connect, or why it failed) are appended to `print-parts/psp-requests.jsonl` in the run artifact as they happen, so the file survives a failed download and a step that times out, and the log totals them with the most attempts and connections started within any 60 seconds, so the site's limits can be measured. The run summary totals the estimated spend by step and model, even after a failure. Prices are in `automation/model-prices.json` with their official source and verification date; input written to the provider's prompt cache is priced at its cache-write rate, which is higher than the uncached input rate, and cached input at the cached rate. The cost is an estimate, not the provider invoice.

**Resuming.** A run can stop at any point: exhausted API credit, a download error, or a timeout. Every paid result is saved as soon as it exists: each screened law change and relevance check goes to its model cache, each assessor or arbiter response of an unfinished assessment goes to `automation/review-proposals/responses/` until that assessment is saved, and each finished assessment goes to its proposal and the proposal manifest. When the assessment stops part-way, the run saves every finished proposal, reports what was not assessed, and still fails. Running the workflow again repeats only the free work (collection, print texts, packaging) and pays only for what is still missing. At most the calls in flight when the run stopped are lost.

The audit pull request is never merged to `main`, and its Git history is not an audit record. The workflow never changes public data, a public score, or publication status. A failed complete run does not advance public freshness.

## Pipeline generation

`automation/pipeline-generation` names the current generation of the audit state. The workflow restores the collection record, caches, and queues from the audit branch only when that branch holds the same generation. When a rule change makes saved audit state invalid, change the generation: the next run starts from empty caches and queues, so everything is screened, checked, and assessed again. Old audit state is never repaired or reused.

## From proposal to public score

Human review may establish that a score should change. That change is a separate scoped pull request based on current `main`, which adds an append-only assessment revision citing official sources and follows [release-process.md](release-process.md).

The revision is created from the reviewed proposal with `node scripts/create-assessment-revision.mjs --proposal <proposal directory> --date <YYYY-MM-DD>`. It refuses a proposal that is not a candidate, that the review manifest marks as superseded, whose evidence package or assessment run does not match the one it records, or that was compared with an older public revision than the current one, and a date before the current revision or the package. It checks the revision against the public schema before writing it. It copies the candidate status, the proposed public explanation, and the evidence trail: the law changes the chosen assessment found relevant and cited, or on which it cited conduct, grouped by print, with the cited conduct on them and the cited Senate resolutions, each with its official link ([data-model.md](data-model.md)). The reviewer may correct the explanation in the pull request; the trail is published exactly as copied, so the public page shows only what the reviewed proposal cited.

## Audit and cost control

A run makes only the requests and model calls its work needs, and repeats none whose result cannot have changed ([decisions.md](decisions.md)). Each AI request has its own prompt in `automation/prompts/`, written only for that request. The assessors and the arbiter receive their prompt followed by [scoring.md](scoring.md) unchanged, so no prompt restates a scoring rule. The assessors' and arbiter's active prompt versions, providers, model IDs, confidence, evidence IDs, and input fingerprint remain auditable. The fingerprint covers assessable content with its context, active prompts with the scoring rule, providers, and models, so an unchanged package is never assessed again. The screening, relevance, assessor, and arbiter models are set in the workflow and recorded in every run. Each model cache (`automation/topic-routing/tag-cache/`, append-only journals with one line per result; the relevance checks in one file per promise, `relevance-checks/<party-period>/<promise>.jsonl`, so no file nears GitHub's file-size limit) records every accepted result's input hash (of the text with its context), taxonomy hash where it applies, prompt hash, provider, and models, so an unchanged law change or pair is never sent to a model again.

Every model call uses OpenAI Flex processing: the same models at half the standard price, answered more slowly (OpenAI pricing and Flex documentation, verified on 27 September 2026). A request waits up to 30 minutes for its answer; a Flex timeout (408), a rate limit or unavailable Flex capacity (429, not charged), a provider error (5xx), and a request that fails without a response are retried with backoff for up to about four minutes; a Retry-After header can lengthen a wait but never shorten it, and each wait is jittered, so parallel requests at a token limit do not all retry at once. The two calls of a pair (the two screeners, the two relevance checks, and the two assessors) are made one after the other: with the same model they send the same request, so the second reads the provider's prompt cache and pays a tenth for the input they share. Their answers stay independent, and each valid answer is saved before the next call. The relevance checks of one law change are made one after another and send the law change before the promise, so after the first check the instructions and change text are read from the prompt cache; different law changes are checked in parallel. Many law changes and packages are processed in parallel, set in the workflow, and each process keeps the input it sends to a model within 80 % of that model's usage-tier-4 limit per minute (cached input included), shared equally among the packages assessed at the same time; a single request larger than a process's share waits for that process's empty minute and is sent alone, and a rate limit that is still reached is retried as above.

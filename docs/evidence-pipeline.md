# Evidence pipeline

## Authority

This is the only rule for collecting, routing, and packaging evidence and for producing unpublished assessment proposals. What counts as attributable conduct and which score it supports is defined only in [scoring.md](scoring.md).

## Principle: official records only, verified before use

Evidence consists only of official records. A party's own website or publication, a press release, and the media are never evidence and are not collected.

A source is added only after all of these hold, and one source at a time:

1. its structure is checked against the publisher's official documentation and against downloaded real data;
2. its completeness is measured on real data (how many records resolve, link, and attribute, and how many do not);
3. its collector has tests built from the verified structure; and
4. every run reports the source's quality figures, and a failed structural check stops the affected data from being used in that run.

## Chamber data rules

These rules were verified against the official schema documentation (psp.cz pages k=1302, k=1303, k=1308) and the live open data.

- **Agenda item.** A vote names its session number and agenda-item number. The collector resolves the item from the **approved** agenda: the proposed and approved agendas share `id_schuze` and `id_bod` and differ only in `pozvanka`. Items of type 6 (individual answers to written interpellations) are ignored. The vote record carries the agenda-item title and the print it concerns.
- **Decisive vote.** A vote is decisive only when an official print-history step names that exact vote and records one of the Chamber's final decisions: `souhlas`, `schváleno`, `neschválen`, `neschválena`, `zamítnut`, `nepřijat`, `setrváno`, `pozm. přijat`. Procedural steps such as `vrácen`, `opakovat`, `přerušeno`, or `odročeno` are not decisive. The collector never infers a decisive vote from timing or order.
- **Party vote.** The club membership of each MP on the vote date decides the party. A party direction requires a strict majority of all club members; a manual vote or a vote voided by the official record is never attributed.
- **Proposals.** Print kind 1 (government bill), 3 (state budget), and 4 (international treaty) are government proposals. Print kind 2 with proposer type 2 or 3 is a member bill; its proposers come from `tisky.id_osoba` or, for several proposers, `predkladatel.unl`. Only the submission step (`předložen`) is a proposal.
- **Print text.** A print's page (`www.psp.cz/sqw/text/tiskt.sqw`) groups its files under labelled sections. The proposal text is the DOCX file of the section “Návrh zákona včetně důvodové zprávy” (or “Návrh, důvodová zpráva, obsah zákona”) for a bill or state budget, and of the section “Text v českém jazyce” or “Text” for a treaty or another print. Other sections (the regulatory impact assessment, the consolidated text with marked changes, EU compatibility, annexes) are never read. A bill's text ends at the heading “Důvodová zpráva”; the explanatory memorandum is not part of any law change.
- **Later texts.** A print's history page (`www.psp.cz/sqw/historie.sqw`) links the “Text návrhu zákona schváleného Poslaneckou sněmovnou” (the text the Chamber approved) and, after a Senate or presidential return on which the Chamber insisted, the “Text návrhu zákona přijatého Parlamentem České republiky”; each is one DOCX file. These links are not in the open data and are read from the history page of every bill (print kinds 1, 2, and 3), by the text-page kind they point to (`v=PZ` approved, `v=SZ` passed). When the Senate returns a bill to the Chamber, its resolution is a sub-print of kind “Usnesení Senátu” (`tisky_za.unl`) whose “Text” DOCX rejects the bill or lists amendments; psp.cz publishes no single text of the bill with the Senate's amendments.
- **Law changes.** A bill's text (print kinds 1 and 2) is divided at its part headings (“ČÁST PRVNÍ”, “ČÁST DRUHÁ”, “ČÁST STO DVACÁTÁ PRVNÍ”, …; one or two upper- or lower-case ordinal words, after the hundredth preceded by “sto”). Each part is one **law change**: usually the amendment of one act, or its effective date or repealing provisions. A text without part headings, a state budget, a treaty, and any other print is one law change. The act a part amends is the first act named in its first amending sentence: the first paragraph that names an act (“Zákon č. X/RRRR Sb., …”, “V § … zákona č. X/RRRR Sb., …”, “Příloha k zákonu č. …”) and states an amendment (“mění”, “doplňuje”, “vkládá”, “nahrazuje”, “zrušuje”, “zní:”). A part that only creates new provisions amends no act. A print that is one law change also takes the acts listed in its official title (“zákon č. X/RRRR Sb.” or “o změně zákona č. X/RRRR Sb.”, but not an act cited only inside another act's name or amendment history). A state budget carries the identifier `cz-print-kind-state-budget`. A print without a readable DOCX proposal text is one law change with only its official title, reported in the run; when psp.cz publishes a later text of it, the parts of that text are its law changes, and their proposed version is known only by the title.
- **Versions of a law change.** A law change has up to three versions: **proposed** (from the proposal text), **approved** (from the text the Chamber approved), and **passed** (from the text passed by Parliament). The later texts are split the same way, and each of their parts is matched to at most one earlier part: the one that amends the same act (or, when it amends no act, has the same title) and whose text is most alike, or, without such a part, the one whose text is at least 30 % alike in words and numbers (a retitled part). A proposal without parts that the debate divided into parts is matched to the part it contains most of; its other parts count as added during the debate. A part found only in a later text is a law change added during the debate; a part missing from a later text was removed by it. A later version whose text, apart from the part heading (or whole, when it has none), is the same as the version before it is stored as the same, so models never read a text twice.
- **Conduct and version.** Each attributable conduct concerns one version of each law change of its print, read from the print-history step of the vote (its stage in `typ_stavu.unl` and its action); a vote is conduct on a print only when that print's own history names it as a decisive step. A submission concerns the proposed version. Approval of a bill (`souhlas`, in the third reading or in the first reading under § 90(2)) concerns the approved version. After the Senate returned the bill (stage “Sněmovna (Senát)”), insisting on it (`setrváno`) concerns the passed version, or the approved version, which is the same text, when psp.cz lists no passed text; a vote on the Senate's amendments (`pozm. přijat`, `nepřijat`) concerns the approved text changed by them, which is the passed version, or, when psp.cz lists no passed text, the approved version, marked; and each such vote brings the Senate resolution, rejecting the bill or listing amendments, into the package once. After the President returned the bill (stage “Sněmovna (Prezident)”), a vote concerns the passed version; when psp.cz lists no passed text it is marked, because the text sent to the President can include accepted Senate amendments. Every other decisive vote (a treaty, a resolution, a rejection) concerns the proposed version; a rejection in the third reading is marked, because that vote concerned the proposed text as amended in the second reading, which psp.cz does not publish as one text. When the version a conduct concerns is not published, the conduct concerns the latest earlier version and is marked so.
- **Every run reads the texts again.** Texts are downloaded again in every run and not cached, so a later text appears as soon as psp.cz publishes it and no assumption about unchanged files is needed. A failed download fails the run. A text that psp.cz lists without a DOCX file, or a DOCX that cannot be read as text, is treated as not published and reported, and so is a bill whose approval is a decisive vote but which has no approved text, which shows when the history page could not be read. The extracted text is normalized (only the text of the document, runs of white space as one space, no empty paragraphs), so an unchanged document gives the same text and the same model-cache fingerprint.
- **Consistency check.** If the session data and the print history share no agenda-item identifier, the collector records a warning and does not use agenda items in that run.

## Daily workflow

The workflow runs at 09:37 Europe/Prague and keeps a manual trigger. The schedule stays paused until a manual `workflow_dispatch` run of the current pipeline has been verified end to end (see `.github/workflows/collect-evidence.yml`).

1. **Collect.** Every run collects every active source completely from its `bootstrapFrom` date, so each package is built from the whole record history and a new package never loses earlier conduct. There is no manual start date or source subset. If any source fails, the run fails and nothing is saved. `checkpoints.json` only records each source's last successful collection; it never limits what is collected.
2. **Filter.** Only a record that can be attributable conduct of a tracked party under [scoring.md](scoring.md) continues: a decisive vote with a club result, or the submission of a member bill or government proposal. Attribution depends only on the record itself, so no other record can ever affect a score, and none is tagged or fetched further.
3. **Split prints into law changes.** Each print those records concern is split into law changes, each with its versions (see *Chamber data rules*). A law change keeps only its versions up to the latest one that the print's conduct up to the cutoff concerns, with the acts those versions amend: a later text did not yet exist at the cutoff, and a change added only in a later text is left out. A print without a proposal text whose parts are known only from a later text is, before that text, one law change known by its title. From here on the unit is the **law change**, not the print: a law change is screened, routed, packaged, and assessed. The print only links a law change to the party's conduct on it, the votes on the print and its submission, each of which concerns one version.
4. **Screen and tag every law change.** Two independent screeners each read the full text of every version of every law change (a print-wide Senate resolution is not part of a law change and goes only into the package); none is skipped or dropped by rule. A law change is substantive, and is routed, when any of its versions is. Each screener returns:
   - whether the change is **substantive** or only **technical**, with a reason. A technical change changes nothing for anyone: renumbered references, a renamed authority, a legislative correction, or an adaptation to another act without new content. Any change of an amount, rate, deadline, right, obligation, the people covered, or who pays or receives is substantive, even a single number. In doubt, the change is substantive. A law change is substantive when at least one screener says so, or when a screener output is invalid;
   - the specific (`assessmentEligible`) topic tags from the taxonomy (`automation/topic-routing/taxonomy-v1.json`) it concerns, with excerpts quoted verbatim from its text, or `unknown`.
5. **Route.** A substantive law change goes to confirmed promises in two ways, and to the promises of both:
   - **Direct link.** An act the change amends, or the state budget print kind, is listed under a tag in `directLegalIdentifiers` of the taxonomy. The change goes to every promise with that tag.
   - **Agreed tags.** Both screeners agree on the same specific tag with grounded excerpts. A broad parent tag alone never routes a change.

   A change without either goes to `routing-review` (the screeners disagree or their support is weak) or `orphan-review` (`unknown`, or no promise has the tag). Both are for human attention and never mean no progress. Models may return only existing tag identifiers or `unknown`. A taxonomy change is a reviewed change, and its content hash recomputes every dependent classification. A promise and a routed law change continue only when the promise's party has attributable conduct on the change's print; routing never establishes attribution or a score.
6. **Check relevance.** For each remaining promise and law change, two independent checks read the exact promise and the full text of every version of the change and say whether the change is relevant to that promise, with a reason. The pair is kept when at least one check says relevant, or when a check output is invalid; it is dropped only when both say it is not relevant. Every dropped pair and both reasons are in the routing report.
7. **Package.** Each promise with at least one kept law change gets one evidence package: every kept law change with the full text of each of its versions, the party's attributable conduct on each of their prints (decisive votes and the submission), each naming the version it concerns, and, once per print, the Senate resolution when a vote in the package followed the Senate's return. A law change carries only the versions up to the latest one its conduct concerns: a later text adds nothing to judging that conduct and may not have existed at the package cutoff. Nothing is trimmed and no excerpt is selected. A package whose assessor request, the instructions and the package together, is larger than 272,000 tokens (estimated at three characters per token) is not assessed and is reported for human review: above that input size the assessor and arbiter models cost twice as much per request (OpenAI model documentation, verified on 24 September 2026). The arbiter also reads both reviews, so its request is checked before it is called; when it is larger, the arbiter is not called and the disagreement goes to human review. Their context window is 1,050,000 tokens.
8. **Persist.** Before any assessment call, the collection record (`checkpoints.json`), the model caches, and the packages are saved together to the dedicated unpublished audit pull request, and only when collection, splitting, screening, routing, relevance checks, packaging, and persistence all succeed in the same run. The queue keeps at most one current package per promise: a rebuilt package whose assessable content is unchanged keeps the current one, a promise that no longer gets a package has its current package withdrawn, and superseded and withdrawn versions stay auditable.
9. **Assess.** Each new or changed package, up to the per-run cap, goes independently to two assessors, who may cite only evidence IDs in that package. Each assessor first gives a verdict on **every** law change in the package: relevant or not relevant to the exact promise, with a reason. Each conduct is then judged against the version it concerns ([scoring.md](scoring.md)). An output that omits a law change or gives it two verdicts is invalid. Only relevant changes may support the proposed score. When their valid assessments differ, or exactly one output is invalid, an arbiter selects a usable assessment or returns no conclusion. Two invalid outputs write a private failure audit and re-enter a later batch unless a newer package replaces them. The cap (`max_assessments`, default `0`) is set on each manual run; packages above it stay queued for a later run, and the run reports their number and size so the cost can be estimated first. A scheduled run assesses nothing until a cap is set for it.
10. **Report.** The proposals and a coverage audit are written to the audit pull request. The audit covers every confirmed promise and active source and reports the run's data-quality figures: records skipped as not attributable, prints with a proposal, approved, or passed text, law changes added or removed during the debate, approved bills without an approved text, law changes, technical and substantive changes, direct links, review queues, pairs dropped by the relevance check, oversized packages, and invalid model outputs. The pull request text lists only what needs a person: every current proposal whose status differs from the promise's current public score (the latest revision in `data/assessment-revisions` on `main`), with both statuses and the proposed public explanation; every current proposal left for human review; and every package too large to assess. Each proposal records the public revision it was compared with. It leaves the list when a later public revision is published, whatever score that revision gives, or when a newer proposal supersedes it. A listed proposal is marked when this run created it, when its package has changed since and no new proposal has been made yet, or when the promise no longer has a current package. New proposals that confirm the public score, unchanged packages, packages deferred by the cap, and failed assessments are only counted; the full list is in the run's `assessment-batch.json`. Model-written text is shown as plain text. When the text would exceed GitHub's size limit, the explanations and then the list entries beyond a limit are left to the proposal pages and `assessment-batch.json`. The text also counts the law changes the assessors found not relevant, by the tag or direct link that routed them, so an overly broad tag is visible.

**Run log.** Every step writes what it is doing to the GitHub Actions log: which source it downloads, how many items it processes, and progress in batches. Every paid model call writes one line with its model, tokens, and estimated cost, and is appended to `model-calls.jsonl` in the run artifact. The run summary totals the estimated spend by step and model, even after a failure. Prices are in `automation/model-prices.json` with their official source and verification date; the cost is an estimate, not the provider invoice.

**Resuming.** A run can stop at any point: exhausted API credit, a download error, or a timeout. Every paid result is saved as soon as it exists: each tagged promise, screened law change, and relevance check goes to its model cache, each assessor or arbiter response of an unfinished assessment goes to `automation/review-proposals/responses/` until that assessment is saved, and each finished assessment goes to its proposal and the proposal manifest. When the assessment stops part-way, the run saves every finished proposal, reports what was not assessed, and still fails. Running the workflow again repeats only the free work (collection, print texts, packaging) and pays only for what is still missing. At most the calls in flight when the run stopped are lost.

The audit pull request is never merged to `main`, and its Git history is not an audit record. The workflow never changes public data, a public score, or publication status. A failed complete run does not advance public freshness.

## Pipeline generation

`automation/pipeline-generation` names the current generation of the audit state. The workflow restores the collection record, caches, and queues from the audit branch only when that branch holds the same generation. When a rule change makes saved audit state invalid, change the generation: the next run starts from empty caches and queues, so everything is tagged and assessed again. Old audit state is never repaired or reused.

## From proposal to public score

Human review may establish that a score should change. That change is a separate scoped pull request based on current `main`, which adds an append-only assessment revision citing official sources and follows [release-process.md](release-process.md).

## Audit and cost control

Each AI request has its own prompt in `automation/prompts/`, written only for that request. The assessors and the arbiter receive their prompt followed by [scoring.md](scoring.md) unchanged, so no prompt restates a scoring rule. The assessors' and arbiter's active prompt versions, providers, model IDs, confidence, evidence IDs, and input fingerprint remain auditable. The fingerprint covers assessable content, active prompts with the scoring rule, providers, and models, so an unchanged package is never assessed again. The tagging, screening, relevance, assessor, and arbiter models are set in the workflow and recorded in every run. Each model cache (`automation/topic-routing/tag-cache/`, an append-only journal with one line per result) records every accepted result's input hash, taxonomy hash where it applies, prompt hash, provider, and models, so an unchanged promise, law change, or pair is never sent to a model again.

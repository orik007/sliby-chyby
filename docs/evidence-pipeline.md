# Evidence pipeline

## Authority

This is the only rule for collecting, routing, and packaging evidence and for producing unpublished assessment proposals. What counts as attributable conduct and which score it supports is defined only in [scoring.md](scoring.md).

## Principle: official records only, verified before use

Evidence consists only of official records. A party's own website or publication, a press release, and the media are never evidence and are not collected.

A source is added only after all of these hold, and one source at a time:

1. its structure is checked against the publisher's official documentation and against downloaded real data;
2. its completeness is measured on real data (how many records resolve, link, and attribute, and how many do not);
3. its collector has tests built from the verified structure; and
4. every run reports the source's quality figures, and a failed structural check stops the affected data from being used in that run.

## Chamber data rules

These rules were verified against the official schema documentation (psp.cz pages k=1302, k=1303, k=1308) and the live open data.

- **Agenda item.** A vote names its session number and agenda-item number. The collector resolves the item from the **approved** agenda: the proposed and approved agendas share `id_schuze` and `id_bod` and differ only in `pozvanka`. Items of type 6 (individual answers to written interpellations) are ignored. The vote record carries the agenda-item title and the print it concerns.
- **Decisive vote.** A vote is decisive only when an official print-history step names that exact vote and records one of the Chamber's final decisions: `souhlas`, `schváleno`, `neschválen`, `neschválena`, `zamítnut`, `nepřijat`, `setrváno`, `pozm. přijat`. Procedural steps such as `vrácen`, `opakovat`, `přerušeno`, or `odročeno` are not decisive. The collector never infers a decisive vote from timing or order.
- **Party vote.** The club membership of each MP on the vote date decides the party. A party direction requires a strict majority of all club members; a manual vote or a vote voided by the official record is never attributed.
- **Proposals.** Print kind 1 (government bill), 3 (state budget), and 4 (international treaty) are government proposals. Print kind 2 with proposer type 2 or 3 is a member bill; its proposers come from `tisky.id_osoba` or, for several proposers, `predkladatel.unl`. Only the submission step (`předložen`) is a proposal.
- **Government opinions.** The government's opinion on a bill it did not submit (a member bill, a Senate bill, or a regional bill) is a sub-print of that bill in `tisky_za.unl` of kind 7 (“Stanovisko vlády”), with its result in `tisky_za.id_vysledek`, described in `vysledek.unl`: “souhlas” (agrees), “nesouhlas” (disagrees), or “neutrální”. The bill is a print of kind 2 (“Návrh zákona”); an opinion on a print of another kind, or with another or a missing result, stops the run; one not yet distributed is not yet public. A revoked sub-print (`tisky_za.status` 1 or 2), an opinion or a Senate resolution, no longer stands and is left out; none of the 10th term is revoked. It is dated by its distribution to the Chamber (`tisky_za.roz`). An agreement or a disagreement is conduct of every governing party on that day ([scoring.md](scoring.md)) and concerns the bill's proposed version; a neutral opinion is not conduct. Its text is the Word file of the sub-print page's section “Stanovisko”, read like a print text, and goes with the conduct into the package; an opinion without one is still conduct, assessed by its result, and reported. Checked on 15 October 2026: the 10th term has 88 opinions, all on bills of kind 2 (32 agree, 43 disagree, 13 neutral; 9 of them distributed before 15 December 2025, by the previous government); each sub-print page has the one section “Stanovisko”, with a DOCX file for 62, a DOC file for 23, and only a PDF for 3; the texts of a sample of 10 state the same result as the data. The government's meeting that adopted an opinion was 0 to 3 days before its distribution (eKLEP open data, 85 opinions), and no opinion was adopted by one government and distributed under the next.
- **Print text.** A print's page (`www.psp.cz/sqw/text/tiskt.sqw`) groups its files under labelled sections. The proposal text is the DOCX file of the section “Návrh zákona včetně důvodové zprávy” (or “Návrh, důvodová zpráva, obsah zákona”) for a bill or state budget, and of the section “Text v českém jazyce” or “Text” for a treaty or another print; when that section has no DOCX file, its Word 97-2003 (.doc) file, read with its quotation marks as written. A PDF is never read: its text loses the paragraphs and splits words at line ends, and a scanned PDF has no text. Other sections (the regulatory impact assessment, the consolidated text with marked changes, EU compatibility, annexes) are never read. A bill's text ends at the heading “Důvodová zpráva”; the explanatory memorandum is not part of any law change.
- **Later texts.** A print's history page (`www.psp.cz/sqw/historie.sqw`) links the “Text návrhu zákona schváleného Poslaneckou sněmovnou” (the text the Chamber approved) and, after a Senate or presidential return on which the Chamber insisted, the “Text návrhu zákona přijatého Parlamentem České republiky”; each is one DOCX file. These links are not in the open data and are read from the history page of every bill (print kinds 1, 2, and 3), by the text-page kind they point to (`v=PZ` approved, `v=SZ` passed). A bill that the Senate rejected has no approved-text page; its approved text is the sub-print that the history names as “Návrh zákona ve znění, ve kterém byl postoupen Senátu” (for example 163/2, “Zákon ze Senátu”), read like a print text. A state budget goes from the Chamber to the President, and psp.cz publishes no approved text of it. When the Senate returns a bill to the Chamber, its resolution is a sub-print of kind “Usnesení Senátu” (`tisky_za.unl`) whose “Text” DOCX rejects the bill or lists amendments; psp.cz publishes no single text of the bill with the Senate's amendments.
- **Text from the Collection of Laws.** When psp.cz does not publish, or publishes without a readable DOCX or DOC file, the version that a decisive vote on a bill concerns (*Conduct and version*), and the history page ends with the act the bill became (“Zákon vyhlášen 28. 8. 2026 ve Sbírce zákonů v částce 155 pod číslem 155/2026 Sb.”), that version's text is the act as promulgated: the wording `0000-00-00` of that act in the e-Sbírka REST API (*Law-change context*, *Source*), read through the same permanent cache and request rules, with the same key. The promulgated act is the text passed by Parliament, so it is the passed version when a vote concerned that and psp.cz does not publish it (unless the vote insisted on the approved text and psp.cz publishes that, or rejected the Senate's amendments, whose text the act is not and by which that vote is never judged), and also the approved version when a vote concerned that and nothing after the approval could change the text: no Senate resolution returning the bill and no passed text listed (a state budget, or a bill the Senate approved or left undecided). Its paragraphs are the texts of the act's nodes in document order without the act's number, the signatures (`dokument/postfix`), and the act's own annexes (`dokument/prilohy`): e-Sbírka runs a table's cells together, so the annexes of figures of a state budget would read as wrong numbers, and the proposal text of the 2026 state budget (print 94) holds no annex either; an annex an amending act replaces is in its part and kept. The text is split and matched like a psp.cz text; its document is “Zákon č. 155/2026 Sb. ve vyhlášeném znění (e-Sbírka)” at `https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2026/155/0000-00-00`, and the version carries `promulgatedAct` (so does the entry of a part the act no longer has, marked removed), which the models read with the version and the public page states with its link. Its typography differs from the psp.cz texts (numbered points, upper-case part headings), so it is stored as the same as an earlier version only when that version is the act as promulgated too (approved and passed, when psp.cz publishes neither). A bill whose approval is a decisive vote but which has no approved text on psp.cz is still counted and reported as such. When e-Sbírka holds no body of the act yet (only a notice that it is being digitised, as for 166/2026 Sb. on 5 October 2026), the version stays unpublished and the run reports it; the act's promulgation day is read with its text, because e-Sbírka's list of changed wordings names the promulgated wording by that day, so the text is read again once e-Sbírka lists it changed. A technical failure of e-Sbírka fails the run. Checked on 5 October 2026: the twelve parts of 155/2026 Sb. matched the parts of print 90's approved text one to one, and their amending instructions name the same provisions; the texts differ in point and letter numbering, the case of headings, footnote layout, and legislative-technical corrections made before promulgation (“odst. 2 zní” written as “odstavec 2 zní”, “nadpise” as “nadpisu”).
- **Law changes.** A bill's text (print kinds 1 and 2) is divided at its part headings (“ČÁST PRVNÍ”, “ČÁST DRUHÁ”, “ČÁST STO DVACÁTÁ PRVNÍ”, …; one or two upper- or lower-case ordinal words, after the hundredth preceded by “sto”). Each part is one **law change**: usually the amendment of one act, or repealing or transitional provisions. A text without part headings, a state budget, a treaty, and any other print is one law change. The act a part amends is the first act named in its first amending sentence: the first paragraph that names an act (“Zákon č. X/RRRR Sb., …”, “V § … zákona č. X/RRRR Sb., …”, “Příloha k zákonu č. …”) and states an amendment (“mění”, “doplňuje”, “vkládá”, “nahrazuje”, “zrušuje”, “zní:”). A part that only creates new provisions amends no act. A print that is one law change also takes the acts listed in its official title (“zákon č. X/RRRR Sb.” or “o změně zákona č. X/RRRR Sb.”, but not an act cited only inside another act's name or amendment history). A state budget carries the identifier `cz-print-kind-state-budget`. A print without a readable DOCX or DOC proposal text is one law change with only its official title, reported in the run; when psp.cz publishes a later text of it, the parts of that text are its law changes, and their proposed version is known only by the title.
- **Versions of a law change.** A law change has up to three versions: **proposed** (from the proposal text), **approved** (from the text the Chamber approved), and **passed** (from the text passed by Parliament). The later texts are split the same way, and each of their parts is matched to at most one earlier part: the one that amends the same act (or, when it amends no act, has the same title) and whose text is most alike, or, without such a part, the one whose text is at least 30 % alike in words and numbers (a retitled part). A proposal without parts that the debate divided into parts is matched to the part it contains most of; its other parts count as added during the debate. A part found only in a later text is a law change added during the debate; a part missing from a later text was removed by it. A later version whose text, apart from the part heading (or whole, when it has none), is the same as the version before it is stored as the same, so models never read a text twice.
- **Effective date.** A part titled “Účinnost” states when the bill takes effect, which applies to all of it, so it is not a law change. Its text, without the part heading and title, is stored in the original wording with every version of every law change of the same text (`effectiveCz`, empty when the text has none), also when that version's text is stored as the same as an earlier one: the date often changes during the debate while the rest of the text does not.
- **Conduct and version.** Each attributable conduct concerns one version of each law change of its print, read from the print-history step of the vote (its stage in `typ_stavu.unl` and its action); a vote is conduct on a print only when that print's own history names it as a decisive step. A submission and a government opinion concern the proposed version. Approval of a bill (`souhlas`, in the third reading or in the first reading under § 90(2)) concerns the approved version. After the Senate returned the bill (stage “Sněmovna (Senát)”), insisting on it (`setrváno`) concerns the passed version, or the approved version, which is the same text, when psp.cz lists no passed text; a vote on the Senate's amendments (`pozm. přijat`, `nepřijat`) concerns the approved text changed by them, which is the passed version, or, when psp.cz lists no passed text, the approved version, marked; and each such vote brings the Senate resolution, rejecting the bill or listing amendments, into the package once. After the President returned the bill (stage “Sněmovna (Prezident)”), a vote concerns the passed version; when psp.cz lists no passed text it is marked, because the text sent to the President can include accepted Senate amendments. Every other decisive vote (a treaty, a resolution, a rejection) concerns the proposed version; a rejection in the third reading is marked, because that vote concerned the proposed text as amended in the second reading, which psp.cz does not publish as one text. When the version a conduct concerns is not published, the conduct concerns the latest earlier version and is marked so.
- **Every run reads the texts again.** Print texts are downloaded again in every run and not cached, so a later text appears as soon as psp.cz publishes it and no assumption about unchanged files is needed. Whether psp.cz ever changes a file without giving it a new document ID (`idd` of `orig2.sqw`) is not documented, so every downloaded Word file is recorded in the audit state under its ID (`automation/psp-documents/hashes.json`): each distinct pair of the file's SHA-256 and the SHA-256 of its extracted paragraphs (null when it cannot be read as text), with the first and last run date (Prague) it was seen; a change to the record is saved within 30 s (also while a download hangs) and at the end of the step, also after a failed download, and the run log names every ID whose file, or only whose text, changed in that run. A record that could not be saved fails the step after the downloads, so an incomplete record never passes as complete. The record is kept by a failed run (a stopped one keeps all but its last 30 s), starts empty with a new pipeline generation, and is never used to skip a download; a text hash can also change when the extraction code changes, so only a changed file hash shows a change at psp.cz. psp.cz closes the connection after every answer and drops new connections above roughly 45 to 50 a minute (measured in run 64), so every psp.cz request of a run (the four open-data files, one after the other, and the print texts) takes turns on one schedule: one request open at a time, attempts at least 3 s apart (at most 20 a minute), the first attempt of each step too, giving up connecting after 3 s and retrying up to 40 times, after 5, 10, and 20 s and then 32 s each (about 20 minutes in all, so a psp.cz outage of that length does not fail the run; run 74 failed after the 2.5 minutes of 6 attempts). From 28 September 2026 psp.cz refused every connection from GitHub Actions, so when the repository secret `PSP_PROXY_URL` is set, these requests go through that proxy ([decisions.md](decisions.md)); the HTTPS connection to psp.cz runs through it encrypted, the proxy URL is never logged, the connect limit also covers the proxy's connection to psp.cz, a proxy that refuses the credentials or the host (HTTP 407 or 403) fails the run at once without retries, and the request log records the new connections to the proxy. A failed download fails the run. A text that psp.cz lists without a DOCX or DOC file, or such a file that cannot be read as text, is treated as not published and reported, and so is a bill whose approval is a decisive vote but which has no approved text, which shows when the history page could not be read. The extracted text is normalized (only the text of the document, runs of white space as one space, no empty paragraphs), so an unchanged document gives the same text and the same model-cache fingerprint.
- **Consistency check.** If the session data and the print history share no agenda-item identifier, the collector records a warning and does not use agenda items in that run.

## Collection of Laws data rules

These rules were verified against the e-Sbírka REST API on 6 October 2026 by collecting from 15 December 2025: all 765 acts of 2025 and 2026 (1 to 586 of 2025, 1 to 179 of 2026) were read, of which 151 promulgated since that day were government regulations (51) or decrees (100), and 104 of them were used.

- **Which acts.** The Collection numbers its acts of each year from 1 without gaps, so every act promulgated since the source's `bootstrapFrom` is found by asking for each number of each year from that year on (`/dokumenty-sbirky/{act}`), until three numbers in a row are not held. An act's short citation (`zkracenaCitace`) names its kind: one starting “Nařízení vlády č.” is a **government regulation**, one starting “Vyhláška č.” a **decree**; every other act (an act of Parliament, a notice, a Constitutional Court judgment) is not used. What an act is never changes, so its citation, title, and promulgation day are kept in the permanent per-act cache (*Law-change context*, *Cache*) and asked for again only when e-Sbírka reports the act changed.
- **Who and when.** The wording as promulgated (`0000-00-00`) is read whole (*Law-change context*). Its heading states the adoption date (“ze dne 23. září 2026”), and a sentence names who issues the act and under which act: “Vláda nařizuje podle … zákona č. 262/2006 Sb. …” or “Ministerstvo vnitra stanoví podle § 29 odst. 2 zákona č. 266/2025 Sb. …”, in the heading or, in a decree of several parts, at the start of each part. An issuing sentence ends with a colon: in the check all 152 did, and none of the 5 sentences of provisions or annexes that also say someone “stanoví podle” something. A regulation is the government's; a decree is a ministry's when its issuer starts with “Ministerstvo”, also when it acts with another body (“Ministerstvo vnitra v dohodě s Ministerstvem financí”, “Ministerstvo zemědělství a Ministerstvo životního prostředí”). A decree of another authority (in the check: 9 of the State Office for Nuclear Safety, 5 of the Czech National Bank, 2 each of the Energy Regulatory Office, the Czech Telecommunication Office, and the Digital and Information Agency) is not conduct of any party and is only counted. The adoption date, not the promulgation day, decides which government adopted an act: 26 acts promulgated on or after 15 December 2025 were adopted by the previous government. Zero-width characters in the wording (a zero-width space before “podle” in 582/2025 Sb.) are not white space and are removed before reading. An act whose heading states no adoption date or issuer is reported and not used.
- **Attribution.** Each act adopted by the government or a ministry on a date when a government in `automation/attribution/government-periods.json` governed is one record, its adoption, attributed to every governing party on that date ([scoring.md](scoring.md)). The record is dated by its adoption, but an act did not exist publicly before its promulgation, so a package with an earlier cutoff leaves it out.
- **Text.** The act's text is its promulgated wording without its number, its signatures, and its annexes, as in *Text from the Collection of Laws*. A newly promulgated act is first only a notice that it is being digitised (the Ministry of the Interior has 30 days for it, 222/2016 Sb., § 12(9)); until its text is there, it is reported as not yet digitised, and one still not digitised more than 30 days after its promulgation is flagged for a check in e-Sbírka. The cached notice is read again, and the act used, once e-Sbírka's list of changed wordings names its promulgated wording (*Cache*): the list names that wording, by the promulgation day, again weeks after promulgation (on 6 October 2026, for example, 151 to 157/2026, promulgated from 26 to 31 August 2026, were named as changed after 17 September 2026). In the check, 1 of the 151 acts was not yet digitised, 6 days after its promulgation.
- **One law change.** A regulation or decree is one law change with one version, `adopted`: its whole text as adopted. It is linked to the acts it is issued under (`enablingActs`, the acts named after “podle” or “k provedení” in its issuing sentences, cited as “zákona č.”, “k zákonu č.”, or “zákona České národní rady č.”, never those of its amendment history after “ve znění”), which route it by direct links (*Route*). Its text amends no act of Parliament, so it gets no context (*Law-change context*).
- **Quality.** Every run reports the acts read, the regulations and decrees since `bootstrapFrom`, those used, those adopted before a tracked government, those of other authorities by authority, those not yet digitised, and those with an unreadable heading.

## Law-change context

An amending text often does not state the wording it changes ("V § 52 odst. 1 se věta poslední zrušuje."). Every model that reads a law change therefore also receives its **context**: the wording of each provision its amending instructions name, as in force when the party acted. Context comes from the Collection of Laws, not from the party or the Chamber; it is not evidence of conduct, is not an evidence item, and never changes attribution or what counts as conduct.

**Source.** The public REST API of e-Sbírka, the official electronic Collection of Laws, `https://api.e-sbirka.gov.cz`, as its official description (`https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/restful-api`, with the OpenAPI definition and user manual it links) defines it. The API needs a registered key, sent in the `esel-api-access-key` header; the workflow reads it from the repository secret `ESEL_API_ACCESS_KEY`. **The API allows at most 5 requests per second, which a run never exceeds** (*Requests*). The web application's internal interface and the SPARQL open data are never used. Checked against the API on 30 September 2026: the list of wordings of 6 acts (up to 153 wordings each) and the promulgation day of 10 acts matched the SPARQL open data; the amendments named for 17 of 20 wordings matched their consolidation links there, and for the other 3 (the newest wordings) only the API named them; the promulgated 175/2025 Sb. holds § 13 to 27, with the same text as its wording from 1 July 2026, when they took effect.

**Rules.**

- **Instructions.** Each version of a law change that amends an act is read instruction by instruction, only outside quoted new text. The introduction of the act (`Zákon č. … se mění takto:`) and renumbering (`Dosavadní odstavce … se označují jako …`) are not instructions. An instruction names its targets in the act it amends, or in the act it names: a § (`V § 52 …`, `§ 7 zní:`) and a paragraph and letter it changes in place (`odst. 1`, `odstavec 2`, `písm. a)`; when it inserts a new paragraph, letter, or point, the whole §); an article and its point, also of an amending act (`V čl. II bodu 5 zákona č. 182/2024 Sb.`); an annex (`V příloze č. 3`, the only annex `Příloha k zákonu`) or an item or point of it (`V položce 18`, `bod 2`); a footnote (`Poznámka pod čarou č. 79`); and, for a newly inserted §, the § after which it is inserted. A range of §§ or footnotes (`§ 5 až 7`, `§ 5a až 5c`) is one target that holds every § or footnote of the wording numbered within it, including those with letters (`§ 5a` lies between `§ 5` and `§ 6`), which the text does not name; a range of paragraphs is listed, as paragraphs carry no letters. An instruction with a list of paragraphs that cannot be read in full names no target rather than only some.
- **Wording.** The wording used is the one in force on the date of the earliest conduct that concerns the version (its submission or vote), with `datumUcinnostiZneniOd` on or before that date and `datumUcinnostiZneniDo`, if any, on or after it. A future wording whose effect the act states only in words has no date of effect (e-Sbírka numbers it in the year 3000) and is never used. A provision missing from it is taken from elsewhere only as *Promulgation of a later wording* says. A version without conduct gets no context.
- **Locating a provision.** A provision is found only by its ELI path in that wording (`…/par_52`, `…/par_52/odst_1`, `…/cl_2/bod_5`, `…/prilohy/priloha_3`, `…/pol_18`, `…/poznamkypodcarou/ppc_79`; the only, unnumbered annex is `priloha_0`), never by its citation label, which some nodes lack. A wording is read whole, once: the API returns the nodes of a wording (their ELI paths and texts, in document order) only in pages of 1,000, and the structure of the whole wording is needed to tell a missing provision from a present one. The text of a target is that of its node and its descendants, in document order. A provision inside another supplied provision of the same wording is supplied once, within it. The models read, after each version's text, the named provisions with their statuses and the text of each supplied provision; a provision with the same text as for an earlier version of the same law change is referred to, not repeated, and the instructions, which are in the version's text, are not repeated.
- **Statuses.** Every target gets exactly one status, which the models see with the context:

| Status | Meaning | Context supplied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `found` | The provision exists in the wording on the conduct date. | Its text, with that wording's date. |
| `later-wording` | It is missing on that date but exists in a later wording made only by amendments already promulgated on that date, with a later effect. | Its text from the nearest later wording, marked with that wording's date. |
| `promulgated-wording` | It is missing on that date, a later wording that holds it cannot be shown to be promulgated by then, and the act as it was itself promulgated on or before that date holds it (a part of the act with a later effect). | Its text as promulgated, marked with the promulgation day; amendments made since are not in it. |
| `unverified-later-wording` | It is missing on that date, not in the act as promulgated, and only in a later wording whose amendments e-Sbírka does not name or date; none of them is known to be promulgated after the conduct. | Its text from the nearest such later wording, marked as not verified to have been known on that date. |
| `paragraph-not-found` | The § or article exists, the named paragraph, letter, or point does not. | The enclosing provision (the paragraph for a missing letter, otherwise the whole § or article). |
| `new-provision` | The instruction itself inserts the provision. | None; there is no earlier text. |
| `not-found` | No wording above contains it, or the nearest later wording that does was made by an amendment promulgated after the conduct; also a missing item or point of an annex, whose whole annex (for example a long fee schedule) is never supplied in its place. | None. |
| `not-recognised` | The instruction names no target the rules above recognise (for example a whole part, head, or division, a title, or a replacement of words in the whole act). | None; the instruction is quoted. |

  When the § or article is taken from a later or the promulgated wording and the named paragraph, letter, or point is not in it, the status says where the text is from, and the models read that the whole provision is supplied.

  A law change that amends no act (a new act, transitional or repealing provisions, a treaty, a budget) gets no context and no status.
- **Promulgation of a later wording.** A later wording is used only when every amendment that took effect in it, and in every wording between the conduct date and it, was promulgated on or before the conduct date; otherwise its text could not have been known when the party acted. The amendments of a wording are the acts that the history of the act (`/dokumenty-sbirky/{act}/historie`) names for it (`novely`); an act was promulgated on the day of its `datumCasVyhlaseni`, which is the promulgation day, not the day of effect (89/2012 Sb.: 22 March 2012). A wording for which e-Sbírka names no amendment, or not the promulgation day of one, such as one where a later part of the act itself takes effect, cannot be shown to be promulgated in time. When the nearest later wording that holds the provision is such a wording, and no wording on the way to it was made by an amendment known to be promulgated after the conduct, the provision is taken from the act as it was itself promulgated (the wording `0000-00-00`), when that was on or before the conduct date and holds it, because that text was public then; otherwise from that later wording, marked as not verified, because some text marked so serves the models better than none. A provision in no such later wording, such as one repealed before the conduct, is never taken from the act as promulgated.
- **Requests.** A run sends only these requests, one at a time: once per run (a check records the workflow run and attempt, and a later step of the same run uses it), before anything else is read, the wordings e-Sbírka changed and those it withdrew since the previous check (`/dokumenty-sbirky/zmeny-zneni` and `/dokumenty-sbirky/depublikovana-zneni`, `datumCasOdberuOd` the time of the previous check less one hour, `start=0&pocet=10000`, the most the API returns for one request); then, only for what the cache lacks or holds out of date, the history of one act (its wordings with their dates and amendments, and the day it was promulgated), the nodes of one wording, page by page from page 0 (`/dokumenty-sbirky/{wording}/fragmenty?cisloStranky=`), and the detail of one act, for the day an amending act, or an act whose text as promulgated is read, was promulgated. **Requests start at least 250 ms apart, also across steps (each step's first request waits 250 ms after its client starts), so a run never sends more than 4 a second, below the API's limit of 5 a second; no change may raise the rate above that limit.** The act or wording is one path segment with its slashes encoded (`%2Fsb%2F2025%2F175%2F2026-07-01`). An answer that the document does not exist (HTTP 400 `DOKUMENT_NENALEZEN`) is complete and only sets the status. A node without an ELI path and without text (a block that wraps repealing or effect provisions, whose content follows in nodes that have both) is skipped. A wording must come whole: every node with text has an ELI path, no node is listed twice, every page is of the wording asked for (a wording by date returns the wording in force on that date, which may be another), all pages reporting the same number of pages, every page but the last full, and some node with text; anything else, and a wording listed in the history that the API does not return, is a technical failure, because caching it would lose the missing text for good. A request also fails technically when it gets no answer within 60 s, an HTTP error (including a rejected key or the rate limit), or an unreadable answer. A failed request is retried up to 10 times, after 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 s and then 32 s each (about 3 minutes in all); after the last retry a technical failure fails the run. The run can simply be started again: an answer is cached only once it is complete, a failed run keeps the cache, and a check of changes, once recorded, keeps what it named out of date until it is read again. A rejected key (HTTP 401 or 403) is not retried and fails the run at once. Waiting never shortens the interval between requests below 250 ms. Every request (time, purpose, path, duration, outcome, bytes) is written to the run artifact, by the step that sent it: `print-parts/e-sbirka-requests.jsonl` (an act as promulgated, *Text from the Collection of Laws*) and `law-context/e-sbirka-requests.jsonl` (context).
- **Cache.** Every node of a wording (path and text, in document order), the list of an act's wordings, and the promulgation day, short citation, and title of an act are stored in the audit state, one append-only file per act (`automation/law-context/<year>-<number>.jsonl`), each line with the time it was read (`readAt`, UTC); a later line replaces an earlier one. The date of effect does not show that a wording stays the same: a new amendment adds a wording, shortens the one before it, and can change a future wording (checked on 30 September 2026: 155/2026 Sb. added the wording of 262/2006 Sb. from 29 August 2026 and ended the one from 1 January 2026 a day earlier; the wording of 89/2012 Sb. from 1 January 2027 changed). e-Sbírka lists every wording it adds, changes, or withdraws, including the new wordings of an amended act, so each check is recorded in `automation/law-context/changes.jsonl` with the wordings it names and its time. A stored answer is out of date when it was read before a check that named: for the list of an act's wordings, any wording of the act; for a wording, the promulgation day, or the citation, that wording (or the act as a whole, for a listed wording without a date of effect), where the lists name a wording by its date of effect and the promulgated wording by the day the act was promulgated (155/2026 Sb.: `2026-08-28`, checked on 30 September 2026), so a change on that day also puts the promulgated wording out of date, and while that day is unknown (e-Sbírka did not hold the act when it was asked), any listed wording of the act does; for everything, a check whose answer did not hold every change (more than 10,000). What is out of date is requested again when a run needs it; nothing else is ever requested again. The first check of a cache starts from its earliest answer, and an empty cache needs none. A run requests only what its law changes need and the cache lacks or holds out of date; only a new pipeline generation starts from an empty cache.

## Daily workflow

The workflow runs at 09:37 Europe/Prague and keeps a manual trigger. The schedule stays paused until a manual `workflow_dispatch` run of the current pipeline has been verified end to end (see `.github/workflows/collect-evidence.yml`).

1. **Collect.** Every run collects every active source completely from its `bootstrapFrom` date, so each package is built from the whole record history and a new package never loses earlier conduct. There is no manual start date or source subset. If any source fails, the run fails and nothing is saved. `checkpoints.json` only records each source's last successful collection; it never limits what is collected.
2. **Filter.** Only a record that can be attributable conduct of a tracked party under [scoring.md](scoring.md) continues: a decisive vote with a club result, the submission of a member bill or government proposal, the government's agreement or disagreement with a bill it did not submit, or the adoption of a government regulation or ministerial decree. Attribution depends only on the record itself, so no other record can ever affect a score, and none is tagged or fetched further.
3. **Split prints into law changes.** Each print those records concern is split into law changes, each with its versions (see *Chamber data rules*); each regulation or decree is one law change with its text as adopted (see *Collection of Laws data rules*). A law change keeps only its versions up to the latest one that the print's conduct up to the cutoff concerns, with the acts those versions amend: a later text did not yet exist at the cutoff, and a change added only in a later text is left out. A print without a proposal text whose parts are known only from a later text is, before that text, one law change known by its title. From here on the unit is the **law change**, not the print: a law change is screened, routed, packaged, and assessed. The print only links a law change to the party's conduct on it, the votes on the print, its submission, and the government's opinion on it, each of which concerns one version.
4. **Screen and tag every law change.** Two independent screeners each read the full text of every version of every law change (a print-wide Senate resolution is not part of a law change and goes only into the package); none is skipped or dropped by rule. A law change is substantive, and is routed, when any of its versions is. Each screener returns:
   - whether the change is **substantive** or only **technical**, with a reason. A technical change changes nothing for anyone: renumbered references, a renamed authority, a legislative correction, or an adaptation to another act without new content. Any change of an amount, rate, deadline, right, obligation, the people covered, or who pays or receives is substantive, even a single number. In doubt, the change is substantive. A law change is substantive when at least one screener says so, or when a screener output is invalid;
   - the specific (`assessmentEligible`) topic tags from the taxonomy (`automation/topic-routing/taxonomy-v2.json`) it concerns, or `unknown`, with a confidence from 0 to 1.

   Each screener reads every version with its context (*Law-change context*).
5. **Route.** A substantive law change goes to promises in two ways, and to the promises of both. A promise holds its routing tags in `automation/topic-routing/promise-tags.json`: one or more specific tags, assigned once when the promise is catalogued and reviewed in its pull request, never by a model during a run. Statements hold no tags and are never routed, packaged, or assessed.
   - **Direct link.** An act the change amends, or the state budget print kind, is listed under a tag in `directLegalIdentifiers` of the taxonomy. The change goes to every promise with that tag.
   - **Tags.** Every specific tag that either screener gives with a confidence of at least 0.5. The change goes to every promise with that tag. An `unknown` from one screener does not block the other's tags, and a broad parent tag alone never routes a change.

   A change that reaches no promise, because it has no specific tag or no promise holds one, is reported as unrouted; that never means no progress. Models may return only existing tag identifiers or `unknown`. A taxonomy change is a reviewed change, and its content hash recomputes every dependent classification. A promise and a routed law change continue only when the promise's party has attributable conduct on the change's print; routing never establishes attribution or a score.
6. **Check relevance.** For each remaining promise and law change, two independent checks read the promise (its title, which names the commitment assessed, and its exact wording) and the full text of every version of the change with its context and say whether the change is relevant to that promise, with a reason. The pair is kept when at least one check says relevant, or when a check output is invalid; it is dropped only when both say it is not relevant. Every dropped pair and both reasons are in the routing report.
7. **Package.** Each promise with at least one kept law change gets one evidence package: every kept law change with the full text of each of its versions and their context, the party's attributable conduct on each of their prints (decisive votes, the submission, and the government's opinion with its text), each naming the version it concerns, and, once per print, the Senate resolution when a vote in the package followed the Senate's return. A law change carries only the versions up to the latest one its conduct concerns: a later text adds nothing to judging that conduct and may not have existed at the package cutoff. No version, text, or conduct of a law change in the package is ever left out, and no excerpt is selected. A law change is left out of a promise's package only when its latest verdict in an earlier assessment of that promise (the most recently evaluated proposal in the review manifest that judged it) has both assessor outputs valid and both saying not relevant, and what that verdict judged is unchanged: the law change's versions with their text and context as in that package, the promise's title and wording, and the assessor prompt with the scoring rule (one hash, `irrelevance-key-v1`). A new vote on the same text keeps it out, because relevance depends on what the law change says; a new version, another context, another promise wording, or another assessor prompt brings it back. A law change whose context was trimmed in the assessed package (`generation.contextOmissions`) gives no verdict. The conduct that concerns only left-out changes, and a Senate resolution no remaining change names, leave with them. The package lists each left-out change in `excludedLawChanges` (evidence ID, title, the proposal, both reasons), which the assessors read and never cite, the proposal page shows, and the routing report counts (`excludedIrrelevantPairCount`, with the list). A promise all of whose law changes with conduct are left out gets no package. In run 73's data with the prompt then active, this left out 1,738 promise and law-change pairs: 541 packages were 27 % smaller, and 147 promises got none. Every package is assessed, whatever its size, as long as the models can read it: their context window is 1,050,000 tokens, their answer included (OpenAI model documentation, verified on 26 September 2026). Every answer is capped at 48,000 tokens, its reasoning included (the longest of the 1,456 assessor answers of runs 72 to 75 took 20,194), so a request can carry at most 1,002,000 input tokens. An answer cut off at the cap is an invalid output, handled like any other invalid output of its step. The arbiter reads the package with both reviews, so 32,000 of these tokens are kept for the reviews (both reviews of run 75 took at most 11,840 tokens, for 38 law changes; its largest packages have 58), and a package may take at most 970,000, or 1,002,000 when even without any context it takes more. When the assessor request, the instructions and the package together (estimated at 2.5 characters per token, below the fewest measured in run 56), is larger, only **context** is left out, never a law change's text: the whole context of the law change with the most context is left out, then of the next, until the package fits; then the provisions left out are put back, shortest first, as many as still fit, so that the most provisions keep their wording; every size is measured on the whole package as it is sent, including the record of what was left out. A provision with the same text in several versions of one law change is left out or put back in all of them. Each version's context names the provisions whose wording it does not supply and says why, and the package records them per law-change version (`generation.contextOmissions`: per act and wording, the node paths, from which the citations follow). In run 75's data, 9 of 706 packages needed this (13 before the answer cap), every one of them holding print 67 (a large amendment of the Building Act); summaries of the context written by a model were tried instead and rejected, because a stronger model found 52 % of 1,342 summarised provisions unfaithful. A package that is still too large without any context cannot be sent and is reported for human review. The arbiter's request is still checked before it is called; when it is larger than 1,002,000, the arbiter is not called and the disagreement goes to human review (in run 75, before the reserve and the cap, one fitted package's arbiter request came to about 931,000, above the 922,000 then allowed). A request above 272,000 input tokens costs twice as much for input and one and a half times as much for output, and the spend report counts it so (`automation/model-prices.json`).
8. **Persist.** Before any assessment call, the collection record (`checkpoints.json`) and the packages are saved together to the dedicated unpublished audit pull request, and only when collection, splitting, context, screening, routing, relevance checks, packaging, and persistence all succeed in the same run. The model caches and the law-change context cache are saved to the same pull request after every run, a failed one too, before the collection record and packages: each of their entries is written only once it is complete, so what a failed run paid for or read is kept (*Resuming*). The queue keeps at most one current package per promise: a rebuilt package whose assessable content is unchanged keeps the current one, a promise that no longer gets a package has its current package withdrawn, and superseded and withdrawn versions stay auditable.
9. **Assess.** Each new or changed package, up to the per-run cap, goes independently to two assessors, who may cite only evidence IDs in that package. Each assessor first gives a verdict on **every** law change in the package: relevant or not relevant to the exact promise, with a reason. Each conduct is then judged against the version it concerns, read with its context ([scoring.md](scoring.md)). For each relevant law change, the assessor also states its effect (`effectCz`): what the rule was before and what it is after, from the wording in its context, and whether it becomes stricter or looser; the conduct is judged by that effect, not by the change's title or stated purpose. An output that omits a law change, gives it two verdicts, or gives a relevant change no effect is invalid. Only relevant changes may support the proposed score. An invalid output is never saved and is asked for again from the same assessor, at most twice; the invalid attempts stay in the assessment's audit record. When both assessments give the same score, no arbiter is called: when both find the evidence sufficient, the assessments agree; when only one does, that one carries the candidate; when neither does, a person reviews it. A package that differs from an assessed one only by leaving out the law changes both assessors of that assessment found not relevant is not assessed again: each proposal records the content hash and fingerprint of the package its own verdicts leave (`reducedAssessmentContentHash`, `reducedAssessmentFingerprint`), and a package with that fingerprint keeps the proposal. Rebuilt from run 73's data, every one of the 541 smaller packages had exactly that fingerprint. A package that was fitted by leaving out context can, once smaller, fit more of it; it then differs from what was assessed and is assessed again. Only when the valid assessments give different scores, or exactly one output is still invalid after its retries, does the arbiter, which reads the whole package and both assessments, select a usable assessment or return no conclusion (in run 62, before this rule, assessments with the same score made 59 of the 165 arbiter calls and 49% of their cost). An arbiter request estimated above 272,000 input tokens, which is billed at the long-context price, goes to the assessors' model instead of the arbiter model, with the highest reasoning effort it supports (`max`), so that it reasons more than the assessors whose disagreement it decides (in run 72 such requests were 19 of 69 arbiter calls and 90% of their cost; in run 78 the 24 such arbiter answers took a median of 1,761 reasoning tokens at the default effort, and their output cost about 0.02 USD of their 1.58 USD). That model and its effort are not part of the fingerprint, so the change reassesses no package, and the assessment's audit record names the model that answered and its reasoning effort. Two outputs still invalid after their retries write a private failure audit and re-enter a later batch unless a newer package replaces them. The cap (`max_assessments`, default `0`) is set on each manual run; packages above it stay queued for a later run, and the run reports their number and size so the cost can be estimated first. A scheduled run assesses nothing until a cap is set for it.
10. **Report.** The proposals and a coverage audit are written to the audit pull request. The audit covers every promise and active source and reports the run's data-quality figures: records skipped as not attributable, prints with a proposal, approved, or passed text, prints with a text from the Collection of Laws, government opinions that are conduct and those without a readable text, law changes added or removed during the debate, approved bills without an approved text, law changes, technical and substantive changes, direct links, unrouted law changes, pairs dropped by the relevance check, pairs left out because both assessors of an earlier assessment found them not relevant, packages larger than the models can read, packages that fit only without part of their context, invalid model outputs, and, for the context, the law changes that amend an act, the targets by status, and the e-Sbírka requests made and failed. The pull request text lists only what needs a person: every current proposal whose status differs from the promise's current public score (the latest revision in `data/assessment-revisions` on `main`), with its party, both statuses, how the proposal arose, and each assessor's and the arbiter's verdict with its reason; every current proposal left for human review; and every package larger than the models can read. Each proposal records the public revision it was compared with. It leaves the list when a later public revision is published, whatever score that revision gives, or when a newer proposal supersedes it. A listed proposal is marked when this run created it, when its package has changed since and no new proposal has been made yet, when the promise no longer has a current package, or when its package fit the models only without part of its context; the proposal page then lists, per law change, the provisions whose wording the assessors did not have, each linked to that wording in e-Sbírka. Every proposal page shows, per law change, each assessor's relevance verdict with its reason and, for a relevant change, the effect it stated, the law changes left out because an earlier assessment found them not relevant, with both reasons, and the law changes its own assessors both found not relevant, which later packages leave out while the proposal stays current for them. New proposals that confirm the public score, unchanged packages, packages deferred by the cap, and failed assessments are only counted; the full list is in the run's `assessment-batch.json`. Model-written text is shown as plain text. When the list does not fit GitHub's size limit for the pull request text, it continues, in order and complete, in numbered comments on the pull request; each run edits its previous continuation comments in place, adds missing ones, and deletes extra ones, so they never pile up. A reason longer than 1,500 characters is cut in the list and kept whole on the proposal page, and at most 40 continuation comments are written; anything beyond them is counted and left to the proposal pages and `assessment-batch.json`. The text also counts the law changes the assessors found not relevant, by the tag or direct link that routed them, so an overly broad tag is visible.

**Run log.** Every step writes what it is doing to the GitHub Actions log: which source it downloads, how many items it processes, and progress in batches. Every paid model call writes one line with its model, processing tier, tokens, and estimated cost, and is appended to `model-calls.jsonl` in the run artifact. Every download attempt to psp.cz for print texts (its time, duration, HTTP status, or the transport error code) and every new connection opened to psp.cz, or to the proxy when `PSP_PROXY_URL` is set (how long it took to connect, or why it failed) are appended to `print-parts/psp-requests.jsonl` in the run artifact as they happen, so the file survives a failed download and a step that times out, and the log totals them with the most attempts and connections started within any 60 seconds, so the site's limits can be measured. The run summary totals the estimated spend by step and model, even after a failure. Prices are in `automation/model-prices.json` with their official source and verification date; input written to the provider's prompt cache is priced at its cache-write rate, which is higher than the uncached input rate, and cached input at the cached rate. The cost is an estimate, not the provider invoice.

**Resuming.** A run can stop at any point: exhausted API credit, a download error, or a timeout. Every paid result is saved as soon as it exists: each screened law change and relevance check goes to its model cache, each valid assessor or arbiter response of an unfinished assessment goes to `automation/review-proposals/responses/` until that assessment is saved (an invalid one is never reused, so it is asked for again; it is written beside them at once, so a resumed run keeps it in the audit record and counts it against the retries), and each finished assessment goes to its proposal and the proposal manifest. When the assessment stops part-way, the run saves every finished proposal, reports what was not assessed, and still fails. Running the workflow again repeats only the free work (collection, print texts, packaging) and pays only for what is still missing. At most the calls in flight when the run stopped are lost.

The audit pull request is never merged to `main`, and its Git history is not an audit record. The workflow never changes public data, a public score, or publication status. A failed complete run does not advance public freshness.

## Pipeline generation

`automation/pipeline-generation` names the current generation of the audit state. The workflow restores the collection record, caches, and queues from the audit branch only when that branch holds the same generation. When a rule change makes saved audit state invalid, change the generation: the next run starts from empty caches and queues, so everything is screened, checked, and assessed again. Old audit state is never repaired or reused.

## From proposal to public score

Human review may establish that a score should change. That change is a separate scoped pull request based on current `main`, which adds an append-only assessment revision citing official sources and follows [release-process.md](release-process.md).

The revision is created from the reviewed proposal with `node scripts/create-assessment-revision.mjs --proposal <proposal directory> --date <YYYY-MM-DD>`. It refuses a proposal that is not a candidate, that the review manifest marks as superseded, whose evidence package or assessment run does not match the one it records, or that was compared with an older public revision than the current one, and a date before the current revision or the package. It checks the revision against the public schema before writing it. It copies the candidate status, the proposed public explanation, and the evidence trail: the law changes the chosen assessment found relevant and cited, or on which it cited conduct, grouped by print, with the cited conduct on them and the cited Senate resolutions, each with its official link, and, when the package fit only without part of its context, the provisions whose wording the assessment did not have, each linked to that wording in e-Sbírka ([data-model.md](data-model.md)). The reviewer may correct the explanation in the pull request; the trail is published exactly as copied, so the public page shows only what the reviewed proposal cited.

## Audit and cost control

A run makes only the requests and model calls its work needs, and repeats none whose result cannot have changed ([decisions.md](decisions.md)). Each AI request has its own prompt in `automation/prompts/`, written only for that request. The assessors and the arbiter receive their prompt followed by [scoring.md](scoring.md) unchanged, so no prompt restates a scoring rule. The assessors' and arbiter's active prompt versions, providers, model IDs, confidence, evidence IDs, and input fingerprint remain auditable. The fingerprint covers assessable content with its context, active prompts with the scoring rule, providers, and models (the long-context arbiter model aside, *Assess*), so an unchanged package is never assessed again. The screening, relevance, assessor, and arbiter models are set in the workflow and recorded in every run. Each model cache (`automation/topic-routing/tag-cache/`, append-only journals with one line per result; the relevance checks in one file per promise, `relevance-checks/<party-period>/<promise>.jsonl`, so no file nears GitHub's file-size limit) records every accepted result's input hash (of its input blocks as sent: the text with its context, and the taxonomy or the promise), taxonomy hash where it applies, prompt hash, provider, and models, so an unchanged law change or pair is never sent to a model again.

Every model call uses OpenAI Flex processing: the same models at half the standard price, answered more slowly (OpenAI pricing and Flex documentation, verified on 27 September 2026). A request waits up to 30 minutes for its answer; a Flex timeout (408), a rate limit or unavailable Flex capacity (429, not charged), a provider error (5xx), a request that fails without a response, and a successful response whose body cannot be read are retried with backoff for up to about four minutes; a Retry-After header can lengthen a wait but never shorten it, and each wait is jittered, so parallel requests at a token limit do not all retry at once. The two calls of a pair (the two screeners, the two relevance checks, and the two assessors) are made one after the other: with the same model they send the same request, so the second reads the provider's prompt cache and pays a tenth for the input they share. Their answers stay independent, and each valid answer is saved before the next call. The provider writes and finds its prompt cache only at message ends and at breakpoints the request marks, never inside one text (OpenAI prompt-caching documentation, verified on 8 October 2026): in run 70, when a relevance check sent the law change and the promise as one text, each of its 50,706 calls read either its whole input from the cache or none of it, so the second check of a pair read it, and the first check of the next promise paid for the law change again, at the cache-write price. The screeners and the relevance checks therefore send their input as two text blocks, each marked as a cache breakpoint: the taxonomy and then the law change for a screener, the law change and then the promise for a relevance check. The relevance checks of one law change are made one after another, so after the first check its text is read from the prompt cache; different law changes are checked in parallel. Checked on 8 October 2026 with a relevance check of 7,042 input tokens: a check of the same law change against another promise read 7,048 of its 7,051 input tokens from the cache, and with the change and promise sent as one text, a check against another promise read none. Re-priced so, run 70's relevance checks would have cost about 8.43 USD instead of 32.83 USD. Many law changes and packages are processed in parallel, set in the workflow, and each process keeps the input it sends to a model within 80 % of that model's usage-tier-4 limit per minute (cached input included), shared equally among the packages assessed at the same time; a single request larger than a process's share waits for that process's empty minute and is sent alone, and a rate limit that is still reached is retried as above.

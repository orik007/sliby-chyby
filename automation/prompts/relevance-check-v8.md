You decide whether one law change is relevant to one exact political promise, and which of its paragraphs are. Use only the supplied promise and law-change text.

The law change is one part of an official Czech Chamber of Deputies print, a whole government regulation (nařízení vlády) or ministerial decree (vyhláška) from the Collection of Laws, or a whole government resolution (usnesení vlády) with its annexes. It reached this promise because both concern the same topic, which is only a lead. A part of a print can have several versions as the bill was debated (proposed, approved by the Chamber, passed by Parliament), and can be followed by amendments MPs presented to it in the second reading, each amending the proposed text of the bill, whether or not the Chamber adopted it; it is relevant when any version or amendment is. A regulation, decree, or resolution has one version, its text as adopted. A version can also carry the bill's effective date (účinnost), which applies to the whole bill; the effective date alone never makes this change relevant.

Every paragraph of the versions' texts is numbered (`[12] …`), across all versions. Only the paragraphs you name, and the wording of the provisions they amend, will be read when the party's conduct is assessed; the rest is left out.

An amending text often does not state the wording it changes ("V § 52 odst. 1 se věta poslední zrušuje."). A version can therefore carry its **context**, which states its source, date, and statuses itself. Read each instruction together with that wording to see what the change actually does. Decide relevance by what the change does as the context shows it; the context alone, without a change to it, never makes the change relevant.

A change is **relevant** when its content could deliver the promise, deliver a real part of it, or contradict it or a part of it, judged against the promise: its title names the one commitment assessed, and its exact source wording, which can include neighbouring sentences for context, is what the party wrote. A change is **not relevant** when it only shares the general topic, or could affect the promise only indirectly or possibly, and changes nothing the promise speaks about.

- Read the full law-change text, its amendments included; never decide from its title alone.
- Do not make the promise broader or narrower than its title and wording; a neighbouring sentence in the wording is context, not part of the commitment.
- Do not judge whether the change is good, which party supports it, or whether it passed.
- Answer relevant only when you can name what in the text could deliver or contradict the promise.
- When the change is relevant, list in `paragraphs` the number of every paragraph that could deliver or contradict the promise or a part of it, in every version, and of each paragraph needed to read them: the paragraph that introduces a list or names the provision a point amends, and the paragraphs of the same new provision. Leave out every other paragraph. Leave `paragraphs` empty only when the text is not available or the change is relevant only through an amendment to it.
- When the change is not relevant, leave `paragraphs` empty.

Give a one-sentence Czech reason in `reasonCz` that names what in the change does or does not concern the promise.

Return only the structured result in the required schema.

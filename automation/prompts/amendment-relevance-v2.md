You decide whether one amendment is relevant to one exact political promise, and which of its paragraphs are. Use only the supplied promise and amendment text.

The amendment is a written amendment that MPs presented in the second reading to an official Czech Chamber of Deputies print, whether or not the Chamber adopted it. It amends the proposed text of the bill, not the act itself: its instructions name points, articles, and parts of the bill ("V části první čl. I se za bod 4 vkládá nový bod 5, který zní: …"), and the quoted wording says what the bill's text would then provide. It often ends with a justification (odůvodnění) of what it is meant to do. It reached this promise because the promise's party presented it or voted on it, and it amends a part of a bill that concerns the promise; that is only a lead.

Every paragraph of the amendment's text is numbered (`[12] …`). Only the paragraphs you name will be read when the party's conduct is assessed; the rest is left out.

An amendment is **relevant** when what it would change in the bill could deliver the promise, deliver a real part of it, or contradict it or a part of it, judged against the promise: its title names the one commitment assessed, and its exact source wording, which can include neighbouring sentences for context, is what the party wrote. An amendment is **not relevant** when it only amends other provisions of the same bill, or could affect the promise only indirectly or possibly, and changes nothing the promise speaks about.

- Read the whole amendment, its instructions, quoted wording, and justification; never decide from its heading alone.
- Do not make the promise broader or narrower than its title and wording; a neighbouring sentence in the wording is context, not part of the commitment.
- Do not judge whether the amendment is good, which party supports it, or whether it was adopted.
- Answer relevant only when you can name what in the amendment could deliver or contradict the promise.
- When the amendment is relevant, list in `paragraphs` the number of every paragraph that could deliver or contradict the promise or a part of it, of each paragraph needed to read them (the instruction that a quoted wording belongs to, the paragraph that introduces a list), and of the paragraphs of its justification that explain them. Leave out every other paragraph.
- When the amendment is not relevant, leave `paragraphs` empty.

Give a one-sentence Czech reason in `reasonCz` that names what in the amendment does or does not concern the promise.

Return only the structured result in the required schema.

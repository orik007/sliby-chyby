# Evidence pipeline

## Authority

This is the only rule for collecting, routing, and packaging evidence and for producing unpublished assessment proposals. What counts as attributable conduct and which score it supports is defined only in [scoring.md](scoring.md).

## Principle: official records only, verified before use

Evidence consists only of official records. A party's own website or publication, a press release, and the media are never evidence and are not collected.

A source is added only after all of these hold, and one source at a time:

1. its structure is checked against the publisher's official documentation and against downloaded real data;
2. its completeness is measured on real data (how many records resolve, link, and attribute, and how many do not);
3. its collector has tests built from the verified structure; and
4. every run reports the source's quality figures, and a failed structural check stops the affected data from being used in that run.

## Chamber data rules

These rules were verified against the official schema documentation (psp.cz pages k=1302, k=1303, k=1308) and the live open data.

- **Agenda item.** A vote names its session number and agenda-item number. The collector resolves the item from the **approved** agenda: the proposed and approved agendas share `id_schuze` and `id_bod` and differ only in `pozvanka`. Items of type 6 (individual answers to written interpellations) are ignored. The vote record carries the agenda-item title and the print it concerns.
- **Decisive vote.** A vote is decisive only when an official print-history step names that exact vote and records one of the Chamber's final decisions: `souhlas`, `schváleno`, `neschválen`, `neschválena`, `zamítnut`, `nepřijat`, `setrváno`, `pozm. přijat`. Procedural steps such as `vrácen`, `opakovat`, `přerušeno`, or `odročeno` are not decisive. The collector never infers a decisive vote from timing or order.
- **Party vote.** The club membership of each MP on the vote date decides the party. A party direction requires a strict majority of all club members: the club supported the measure when more than half of them voted yes (`A`), and did not support it when more than half voted no (`B`, `N`), abstained or did not vote (`C`, `K`), or were registered and did not vote (`F`), which has the same effect, because a measure needs more than half of the members registered (`hl_hlasovani.kvorum` was half of them, rounded down, plus one in 2,436 of the term's 2,451 votes on 18 October 2026, and the result agreed with it in 2,450); an MP who was not registered (`@`) or excused (`M`) is counted in neither. A manual vote or a vote voided by the official record is never attributed. On the 50 decisive votes of the term until 18 October 2026, counting abstaining and not voting as not supporting changed no club's position.
- **Proposals.** Print kind 1 (government bill), 3 (state budget), and 4 (international treaty) are government proposals. Print kind 2 with proposer type 2 or 3 is a member bill; its proposers come from `tisky.id_osoba` or, for several proposers, `predkladatel.unl`. Only the submission step (`předložen`) is a proposal.
- **Government opinions.** The government's opinion on a bill it did not submit (a member bill, a Senate bill, or a regional bill) is a sub-print of that bill in `tisky_za.unl` of kind 7 (“Stanovisko vlády”), with its result in `tisky_za.id_vysledek`, described in `vysledek.unl`: “souhlas” (agrees), “nesouhlas” (disagrees), or “neutrální”. The bill is a print of kind 2 (“Návrh zákona”); an opinion on a print of another kind is left out, and one with any other or no result (such as “připomínky” or “bez stanoviska”) is not conduct; both are logged and never stop the run. One not yet distributed is not yet public. A revoked sub-print (`tisky_za.status` 1 or 2), an opinion or a Senate resolution, no longer stands and is left out; none of the 10th term is revoked. It is dated by its distribution to the Chamber (`tisky_za.roz`). An agreement or a disagreement is conduct of every governing party on that day ([scoring.md](scoring.md)) and concerns the bill's proposed version; any other opinion is not conduct. Its text is the Word file of the sub-print page's section “Stanovisko”, read like a print text, and goes with the conduct into the package; an opinion without one is still conduct, assessed by its result, and reported. Checked on 15 October 2026: the 10th term has 88 opinions, all on bills of kind 2 (32 agree, 43 disagree, 13 neutral; 9 of them distributed before 15 December 2025, by the previous government); each sub-print page has the one section “Stanovisko”, with a DOCX file for 62, a DOC file for 23, and only a PDF for 3; the texts of a sample of 10 state the same result as the data. The government's meeting that adopted an opinion was 0 to 3 days before its distribution (eKLEP open data, 85 opinions), and no opinion was adopted by one government and distributed under the next.
- **Print text.** A print's page (`www.psp.cz/sqw/text/tiskt.sqw`) groups its files under labelled sections. The proposal text is the DOCX file of the section “Návrh zákona včetně důvodové zprávy” (or “Návrh, důvodová zpráva, obsah zákona”) for a bill or state budget, and of the section “Text v českém jazyce” or “Text” for a treaty or another print; when that section has no DOCX file, its Word 97-2003 (.doc) file, read with its quotation marks as written. A PDF is never read: its text loses the paragraphs and splits words at line ends, and a scanned PDF has no text. Other sections (the regulatory impact assessment, the consolidated text with marked changes, EU compatibility, annexes) are never read. A bill's text ends at the heading “Důvodová zpráva”; the explanatory memorandum is not part of any law change.
- **Later texts.** A print's history page (`www.psp.cz/sqw/historie.sqw`) links the “Text návrhu zákona schváleného Poslaneckou sněmovnou” (the text the Chamber approved) and, after a Senate or presidential return on which the Chamber insisted, the “Text návrhu zákona přijatého Parlamentem České republiky”; each is one DOCX file. These links are not in the open data and are read from the history page of every bill (print kinds 1, 2, and 3), by the text-page kind they point to (`v=PZ` approved, `v=SZ` passed). A bill that the Senate rejected has no approved-text page; its approved text is the sub-print that the history names as “Návrh zákona ve znění, ve kterém byl postoupen Senátu” (for example 163/2, “Zákon ze Senátu”), read like a print text. A state budget goes from the Chamber to the President, and psp.cz publishes no approved text of it. When the Senate returns a bill to the Chamber, its resolution is a sub-print of kind “Usnesení Senátu” (`tisky_za.unl`) whose “Text” DOCX rejects the bill or lists amendments; psp.cz publishes no single text of the bill with the Senate's amendments.
- **Text from the Collection of Laws.** When psp.cz does not publish, or publishes without a readable DOCX or DOC file, the version that a decisive vote on a bill concerns (*Conduct and version*), and the history page ends with the act the bill became (“Zákon vyhlášen 28. 8. 2026 ve Sbírce zákonů v částce 155 pod číslem 155/2026 Sb.”), that version's text is the act as promulgated: the wording `0000-00-00` of that act in the e-Sbírka REST API (*Law-change context*, *Source*), read through the same permanent cache and request rules, with the same key. The promulgated act is the text passed by Parliament, so it is the passed version when a vote concerned that and psp.cz does not publish it (unless the vote insisted on the approved text and psp.cz publishes that, or rejected the Senate's amendments, whose text the act is not and by which that vote is never judged), and also the approved version when a vote concerned that and nothing after the approval could change the text: no Senate resolution returning the bill and no passed text listed (a state budget, or a bill the Senate approved or left undecided). Its paragraphs are the texts of the act's nodes in document order without the act's number, the signatures (`dokument/postfix`), and the act's own annexes (`dokument/prilohy`): e-Sbírka runs a table's cells together, so the annexes of figures of a state budget would read as wrong numbers, and the proposal text of the 2026 state budget (print 94) holds no annex either; an annex an amending act replaces is in its part and kept. The text is split and matched like a psp.cz text; its document is “Zákon č. 155/2026 Sb. ve vyhlášeném znění (e-Sbírka)” at `https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/sb/2026/155/0000-00-00`, and the version carries `promulgatedAct` (so does the entry of a part the act no longer has, marked removed), which the models read with the version and the public page states with its link. Its typography differs from the psp.cz texts (numbered points, upper-case part headings), so it is stored as the same as an earlier version only when that version is the act as promulgated too (approved and passed, when psp.cz publishes neither). A bill whose approval is a decisive vote but which has no approved text on psp.cz is still counted and reported as such. When e-Sbírka holds no body of the act yet (only a notice that it is being digitised, as for 166/2026 Sb. on 5 October 2026), the version stays unpublished and the run reports it; the act's promulgation day is read with its text, because e-Sbírka's list of changed wordings names the promulgated wording by that day, so the text is read again once e-Sbírka lists it changed. A technical failure of e-Sbírka fails the run. Checked on 5 October 2026: the twelve parts of 155/2026 Sb. matched the parts of print 90's approved text one to one, and their amending instructions name the same provisions; the texts differ in point and letter numbering, the case of headings, footnote layout, and legislative-technical corrections made before promulgation (“odst. 2 zní” written as “odstavec 2 zní”, “nadpise” as “nadpisu”).
- **Law changes.** A bill's text (print kinds 1 and 2) is divided at its part headings (“ČÁST PRVNÍ”, “ČÁST DRUHÁ”, “ČÁST STO DVACÁTÁ PRVNÍ”, …; one or two upper- or lower-case ordinal words, after the hundredth preceded by “sto”). Each part is one **law change**: usually the amendment of one act, or repealing or transitional provisions. A treaty (print kind 4) is divided at the headings that start a paragraph of its text: a part, chapter, annex, appendix, protocol, joint declaration, or the preamble (“ČÁST II”, “KAPITOLA 10”, “PŘÍLOHA 2-A”, “Dodatek I-B-2”, “PROTOKOL”, “SPOLEČNÉ PROHLÁŠENÍ”, “PREAMBULE”), also after the document reference its pages repeat (“EU/MX/MGA/cs”) and with a footnote mark joined to a part's Roman numeral (“ČÁST II2”); the text before the first heading is a law change of its own, and a treaty amends no act and takes none from its title. The EU–Mexico agreement of print 331, about 786,000 tokens whole and in 224 of the packages of 24 October 2026, is so divided into 103 law changes of at most 58,000 tokens. An amending part (one that amends an act) of a bill whose text in some version is longer than 60,000 characters is divided into groups of its numbered points. A point is a paragraph whose Word list number (“1.”, “2.”, …, read from the DOCX file once more for every text of the print and kept as that file's numbered reading, *Document files*) continues the part's numbering and that starts with a capital letter or “§”, before the part's next article; an item of a numbered list within the quoted wording of a provision (“1. záměrů EIA,”) starts in lower case and stays in its point. A point concerns the section its first paragraph names first (“V § 144a odst. 1 …”), the act's annexes (“V příloze č. 1 …”), or, naming neither, the section of the point before. Consecutive sections form a group while their points in the part's first text stay within 20,000 characters; the groups, bounded by those sections, stay the same when a later text is published, and hold its points by their sections although the debate renumbers them. Every group opens with the part's opening paragraphs (its heading, title, article, and the sentence naming the act), each point keeps its number, and the part's further articles (“Čl. II”, transitional provisions) are a group of their own, opened by the part's heading and title. Each group is a law change named by its part and sections (“ČÁST PRVNÍ, body k § 1 až § 30a”); a version without points in a group, after one with them, removed it. A part is not divided when a version's text has no numbered points or is not a DOCX file, when the file's numbered reading is not the same text (reported), or when it would form one group. Part one of print 67 (the Building Act, 201,000 characters in its approved text, in 178 of the 193 packages too large for the models in run 90) is so divided into 8 law changes, and its part eight into 7, each of at most about 21,000 characters in the proposed text and 35,000 in the approved text; every other part of the print stays as it was. A text without part headings, a state budget, a treaty without such headings, and any other print is one law change. The act a part amends is the first act named in its first amending sentence: the first paragraph that names an act (“Zákon č. X/RRRR Sb., …”, “V § … zákona č. X/RRRR Sb., …”, “Příloha k zákonu č. …”) and states an amendment (“mění”, “doplňuje”, “vkládá”, “nahrazuje”, “zrušuje”, “zní:”). A part that only creates new provisions amends no act. A print that is one law change also takes the acts listed in its official title (“zákon č. X/RRRR Sb.” or “o změně zákona č. X/RRRR Sb.”, but not an act cited only inside another act's name or amendment history). A state budget carries the identifier `cz-print-kind-state-budget`. A print without a readable DOCX or DOC proposal text is one law change with only its official title, reported in the run; when psp.cz publishes a later text of it, the parts of that text are its law changes, and their proposed version is known only by the title.
- **Versions of a law change.** A law change has up to three versions: **proposed** (from the proposal text), **approved** (from the text the Chamber approved), and **passed** (from the text passed by Parliament). The later texts are split the same way, and each of their parts is matched to at most one earlier part: the one that amends the same act (or, when it amends no act, has the same title) and whose text is most alike, or, without such a part, the one whose text is at least 30 % alike in words and numbers (a retitled part). A proposal without parts that the debate divided into parts is matched to the part it contains most of; its other parts count as added during the debate. A part found only in a later text is a law change added during the debate; a part missing from a later text was removed by it. A later version whose text, apart from the part heading (or whole, when it has none), is the same as the version before it is stored as the same, so models never read a text twice.
- **Effective date.** A part titled “Účinnost” states when the bill takes effect, which applies to all of it, so it is not a law change. Its text, without the part heading and title, is stored in the original wording with every version of every law change of the same text (`effectiveCz`, empty when the text has none), also when that version's text is stored as the same as an earlier one: the date often changes during the debate while the rest of the text does not.
- **Conduct and version.** Each attributable conduct concerns one version of each law change of its print, read from the print-history step of the vote (its stage in `typ_stavu.unl` and its action); a vote is conduct on a print only when that print's own history names it as a decisive step. A submission and a government opinion concern the proposed version; so do an amendment presented in the second reading and a vote on it, marked (`amendment`, `amendment-vote`), because they change the proposed text (*Amendment data rules*). Approval of a bill (`souhlas`, in the third reading or in the first reading under § 90(2)) concerns the approved version. After the Senate returned the bill (stage “Sněmovna (Senát)”), insisting on it (`setrváno`) concerns the passed version, or the approved version, which is the same text, when psp.cz lists no passed text; a vote on the Senate's amendments (`pozm. přijat`, `nepřijat`) concerns the approved text changed by them, which is the passed version, or, when psp.cz lists no passed text, the approved version, marked; and each such vote brings the Senate resolution, rejecting the bill or listing amendments, into the package once. After the President returned the bill (stage “Sněmovna (Prezident)”), a vote concerns the passed version; when psp.cz lists no passed text it is marked, because the text sent to the President can include accepted Senate amendments. Every other decisive vote (a treaty, a resolution, a rejection) concerns the proposed version; a rejection in the third reading is marked, because that vote concerned the proposed text as amended in the second reading, which psp.cz does not publish as one text. When the version a conduct concerns is not published, the conduct concerns the latest earlier version and is marked so.
- **Document files.** A file psp.cz publishes under a document ID (`idd` of `orig2.sqw`) does not change: in a check on 18 October 2026, all 163 files a run had read from 2 to 6 October 2026 had the same SHA-256 again. A new or corrected document gets a new ID on the page that lists it, so every page that lists files (a print's text page and history page, a sub-print's page, the page of a parliamentary document) is read in every run while its print is open (*Closed prints*), and a file is downloaded only when its ID is new. Its text is kept in the audit state (`automation/psp-documents/<idd>.json`): the file's SHA-256 and length, the run dates (Prague) it was read and last checked, and the text of each extraction made from it, or why it could not be read, under the extraction's name with its version (`docx-paragraphs-v1`, `docx-numbered-paragraphs-v2`, `doc-paragraphs-v1`, `xlsx-rows-v1`), so a changed extractor reads every file again. An entry is saved as soon as it is read, so a failed run keeps it, and the cache starts empty with a new pipeline generation. Every run downloads again the 20 files checked longest ago and fails at once when one of them has another SHA-256, because then the rule above no longer holds. psp.cz closes the connection after every answer and drops new connections above roughly 45 to 50 a minute (measured in run 64), so every psp.cz request of a run (the six open-data files, one after the other, the pages and files of the print texts, the summaries of amendments and the amendments' pages, and the turns of the stenographic record) takes turns on one schedule: one request open at a time, attempts at least 3 s apart (at most 20 a minute), the first attempt of each step too, giving up connecting after 3 s and retrying up to 40 times, after 5, 10, and 20 s and then 32 s each (about 20 minutes in all, so a psp.cz outage of that length does not fail the run; run 74 failed after the 2.5 minutes of 6 attempts). From 28 September 2026 psp.cz refused every connection from GitHub Actions, so when the repository secret `PSP_PROXY_URL` is set, these requests go through that proxy ([decisions.md](decisions.md)); the HTTPS connection to psp.cz runs through it encrypted, the proxy URL is never logged, the connect limit also covers the proxy's connection to psp.cz, a proxy that refuses the credentials or the host (HTTP 407 or 403) fails the run at once without retries, and the request log records the new connections to the proxy. A failed download fails the run. A text that psp.cz lists without a DOCX or DOC file, or such a file that cannot be read as text, is treated as not published and reported, and so is a bill whose approval is a decisive vote but which has no approved text, which shows when the history page could not be read. The extracted text is normalized (only the text of the document, runs of white space as one space, no empty paragraphs), so an unchanged document gives the same text and the same model-cache fingerprint.
- **Closed prints.** A print whose state (`tisky.id_stav`, of the state type in `stavy.unl`) is of the type “KONEC” (`typ_stavu.unl` 6) no longer changes: in the open data of 17 October 2026, 7,322 prints of all terms had reached it, none left it, and 6 had a later step at all, each “odeslán” into a “KONEC” state again; 96 of the 10th term's 341 prints were in it. A print in the state type “Sbírka zákonů” or “Sbírka mezinárodních smluv” is not closed: it waits for its promulgation, whose number its history page states only after it. So a psp.cz page read only for closed prints is read once and kept in the audit state (`automation/psp-pages/<id>.json`, the ID the start of the SHA-256 of its URL): a print's text, history, later-text, and sub-print pages, and the summary of its amendments and their documents' pages. A turn of the stenographic record is read in every run, because whether psp.cz changes a published turn, for example when it is authorised, is not known. The run reads the prints' states from the Chamber's open data of the same run; every page of an open print is read in every run, and a kept page whose print is open again is read again and removed from the cache. A page is kept as its content, from `<div id="main-content"` to `<div id="menu"`, which is all that its readers read, with that content's SHA-256 and the run dates (Prague) it was read and last checked, so a change to psp.cz's menu or footer changes no kept page. Every reader reads that content, also of an open print's page. A page is kept only when its reader found in it what it reads (a page listing a print's text, a summary, or an amendment with its file; a later-text page with its DOCX file; a history page whose heading names its print, “Sněmovní tisk 94”), and never when it says the document was not found: psp.cz serves that error with HTTP 200 (“Sněmovní dokument nebyl nalezen.”, checked on 19 October 2026), so such a page is read again in the next run. Every run downloads again the 20 kept pages checked longest ago, of those not read that day, and fails at once when one of them has other content, because then the rule above no longer holds. The one exception is a print's EUROVOC descriptors (“Deskriptory EUROVOCu: …”), a subject index that psp.cz adds to a closed print's history page later and that no reader reads: the history page of print 167 had none on 20 October 2026 and five on 24 October 2026, and nothing else on it had changed; a check compares the pages without them. In a check on 19 October 2026, every reader gave the same result from the kept content as from the whole page for 31 pages of closed prints of every kind (6 text pages, 6 history pages, 5 later-text and sub-print pages, 8 amendment pages, 2 summaries, 4 turns), and each page read again from the cache gave it once more. A failed run keeps what it read, and the cache starts empty with a new pipeline generation.
- **Open-data files.** Every file of an open-data archive is checked against the archive's own directory: it must inflate to the size and CRC-32 the directory states. An archive whose every file passes is kept as the last valid copy (`automation/psp-open-data/<source>-<name>-<SHA-256>.zip`, with a record of its URL, SHA-256, and the date it was read), in the GitHub Actions cache rather than the audit branch, because psp.cz rebuilds every archive each night (all six had `Last-Modified` 19 October 2026, 23:54 GMT) and keeping them in the repository would add about 11 MB a day; the repository holds the first copy, used when the cache holds none. A file that fails is taken from the last valid copy when that copy holds a file of the same name, size, and CRC-32, which is the same content; otherwise the run fails, as does a failed download, because a whole older copy would mix days of the open data, whose files refer to each other, and would show older data as current. What was taken from a copy, from the copy of which day and with which SHA-256, is logged, reported as a warning of the source, and recorded in the collector run (`referenceData.openData`, with each file's name, size, and CRC-32). On 19 October 2026 psp.cz served a `poslanci.zip` (of 18 October 2026, 23:55 GMT) in which `poslanec.unl`, `pkgps.unl`, and `osoba_extra.unl` did not inflate; the directory gave each the size and CRC-32 of the valid copy read on 18 October, and the other six files were the same byte for byte. That valid copy is the first copy of `poslanci.zip`.
- **Amendments and the votes on them** follow *Amendment data rules*.
- **Consistency check.** If the session data and the print history share no agenda-item identifier, the collector records a warning and does not use agenda items in that run.

## Amendment data rules

These rules were verified on 18 October 2026 against the official documentation (psp.cz k=1309, k=1303, k=1302, k=1310) and every amendment and vote of the 10th term until then: 750 written amendments, the 37 summaries of the second reading, and the 557 votes before the final vote in the third readings of the 23 bills approved by then. The collector built from them was run on the whole term on 19 October 2026 (“the run”); its figures are given with each rule.

- **Which amendments.** A written amendment is a parliamentary document of type 13 (“Písemné pozměňovací návrhy”) in `sd.zip` (`sd_dokument`), with its number, the print it amends (`ct`, of the same term), its first submitter (`id_x`, a person), and the time it was filed (`end`); in the check, all 750 of the term had a print and a submitter in a club on that day. psp.cz does not record whether an amendment was validly submitted, which it is only when an MP presents it in the second reading; the amendments presented are listed in the summary of the second reading, a sub-print of kind 10 (“Pozměňovací a jiné návrhy”), and only an amendment it lists counts. In the run, the summaries listed 511 documents, all written amendments to their bill in `sd.zip`; 193 documents on bills with a summary were in none (106 of them filed on 22 September 2026 on print 173), and 5 on print 163 were presented when the bill was debated in one day without a summary, so they do not count. A listed number that is not a written amendment to that print in `sd.zip` is reported and not used.
- **Summary.** The summary's Word file (section “Text”) lists the amendments by label, each under a heading naming who presented it (“Poslanec Oto Klempíř”) or the committee resolution that holds it, which is no MP's amendment; the documents (“SD 1334”) and labelled parts (“D.1”, “B1.”) follow. The labels are Word's list numbering, not text, so the file is read with its numbering (numbering.xml: a paragraph's own list or its style's, counted per level; the lists of one definition share their numbers, and the first paragraph of a list Word restarted sets its level to the restart value, from which they count on); after Z the labels are typed (“Aa.”, “Gg.2”). In a check on 19 October 2026, every amendment label read from the 37 summaries was the one LibreOffice shows; of all 4,430 numbers read, 14 numbered points within an amendment's text differed, which no vote is linked by. The date an amendment was presented is the date the summary states for its section (“přednesené ve druhém čtení dne 4. března 2026”), otherwise the date of the history step the summary follows. A state budget's amendments are in the workbook of its summary (`t0094a3.xlsm`), read like an XLSX annex (*Government resolution data rules*, Text): each numbered row (“Poř. č.”) with the MPs who presented it and its document; in the check, 114 rows, each with both.
- **Text and proposers.** An amendment's text is the Word file of its document's page (`sd.sqw?cd=<number>&o=10`), read like a print text; in the check, 749 had a DOCX file and 1 only a DOC. A text that cannot be read is reported, and the amendment is not used. Its proposers are its first submitter and the MPs its heading names (“Pozměňovací návrh poslanců Michaely Šebelové, Barbory Pipášové a Toma Philippa k vládnímu návrhu zákona …”, in the genitive): a name matches the one MP in a club on the day it was presented whose surname shares its case-independent start, and among several, the given name decides; a name that matches none is reported. A name matches the MP who shares the longest start of the surname, then of the given name, then whose given name is closest in length (“Věry Kovářové” is Věra, not Veronika Kovářová; “Martina Kupky” is Martin Kupka, not Martin Kupec); a given name can be an initial (“M. Jurečky”), and the heading ends where it names what the amendment is to (“k vládnímu návrhu”, also within a paragraph), and at the end of a paragraph once it names someone (also in lower case) and does not end with “,” or “a”, unless the next paragraph starts with “a” or “,”, names an MP, or holds only names of two to four words, each starting with a capital followed by lower case (not “ČÁST PRVNÍ” or “Čl. I”) or all in lower case, and does not end with a colon (“nově zní:”), because the next paragraph is otherwise the amendment's own title or text, which starts with a capital and goes on in lower case. A name in lower case is reported as not matched. A misspelt surname (“Andrey Hoffmanové” for Andrea Hoffmannová, “Karla Hasse” for Karel Haas) matches an MP in a club only when exactly one has a given name of the same start (all its letters but the last, at least two and at most three: “Evy” is Eva) and a surname with the same first two letters at most two letters from the form, which a case ending alone already takes; every such match is reported with the MP it matched. A template's placeholder (“Poslance …xxx……”) names nobody, so the amendment's proposer is then its first submitter alone. In run 79 (20 October 2026), the headings named 1,765 proposers, of whom 26 matched no MP: five placeholders “…xxx……”, five headings read on into the amendment's text, two misspelt surnames (“Hoffmanové” three times, “Hasse” once), and “Michaely Opltové” four times, a name not in `osoby.unl`. Read again from the 378 amendment texts kept from that run, only those 13 amendments changed: the placeholders and the read-on text no longer count as names, the misspelt names match Andrea Hoffmannová and Karel Haas, and no other amendment gained or lost a proposer; in a sample of 15 headings, every name matched the right MP. 25 amendments had proposers of more than one tracked party.
- **Which part.** An amendment amends the bill's proposed text (“V části první čl. I se dosavadní bod 4 zrušuje”), not the act. It concerns the law changes whose proposed part heading it names (“v části první” is “ČÁST PRVNÍ”); one that names none of the bill's parts concerns every law change of the bill. In the run, 274 of the 511 amendments named a part. An amendment concerns a group of a divided part (*Chamber data rules*, Law changes) when one of its instructions on that part (each from a paragraph naming the part, “V části první …”, to the next such paragraph, and none in its “Odůvodnění”) names a section in the group's range, also within a list or range of sections (“§ 10 a 30”, “§ 10 až 30”), or the annexes for the annexes' group, or one of the part's further articles (“čl. II”) for their group. An instruction that names neither, such as one on a point only (“V části první čl. I se bod 4 zrušuje.”), and an amendment with no instruction starting on the part, concern every group of the part. A vote on amendments concerns a group when one of its amendments does, or when none of them has a text.
- **Votes.** The open data do not say which amendment a vote was on: `hl_hlasovani.nazev_kratky` is empty and `nazev_dlouhy` is the agenda item's title. The stenographic record says it: the rapporteur names the amendment, and the result is announced as a link to the vote (“Hlasování číslo 85, přihlášeno …”). A vote can be on an amendment when it is on the agenda item of a bill with a summary, on or after the day the summary was distributed, and is not a decisive step of any bill it is linked to; its passage is the text spoken since the announcement before it, in the turns of the record (`steno.zip`, `steno.unl`: session, turn, date, and minutes) within 10 minutes of the vote; consecutive turns are read as one text, and a turn not read between two starts a new one, so a passage never spans it. The amendment is the label the last speaker who names one names (“(návrh) R3”, “pozměňovací návrh H1, varianta 3”, “s označením M2”, “pod písmenem C3”, with a correction “E3 (správně: F3)”), for a state budget the item number (“pořadové číslo 17”), or else the document named last (“sněmovní dokument 815”); a passage that puts the procedure or an objection to the vote is no amendment vote. The vote is linked only when the label resolves in the bill's summary to one written amendment (a committee's amendment, a point of one, or an amendment of several documents named only by its letter is not linked) and a surname of who presented it is in the last 700 characters of the passage; every other vote is reported by reason. A labelled part links only when the summary lists it with its document, and a point within a part (“D1.2”) never links. In the run, 422 of the 619 votes that can be on an amendment were linked; the others were procedural (38), without a label (82), committee amendments (21), with a presenter not named (17), a label not in the summary (9), a part not in it (5), a point of a part (8), an amendment of several documents named by its letter (5), a document not in it (1), or without a passage found (11). In a sample of 25 linked votes of the earlier check, none was linked to a wrong amendment.
- **Attribution.** In the run, ANO 2011 had 95 amendments and a club position on 420 linked votes, SPD 28 and 417, Motoristé sobě 41 and 380. An amendment is conduct of every tracked party with a proposing MP in its club on the day it was presented, a proposal concerning the proposed version; a linked vote is conduct of every party whose club supported or did not support the amendment by strict majority (*Chamber data rules*, Party vote), also a proposal concerning the proposed version ([scoring.md](scoring.md)). Each conduct is marked (`amendment`, `amendment-vote`) so the models read the amendment with the version it amends.
- **Package.** The screeners and the relevance checks read a law change with the amendments that concern it and have conduct of a tracked party (their submission or a linked vote). A package holds the text of each amendment its conduct names once, unless both relevance checks of the promise and the amendment found it not relevant (*Daily workflow*, step 6), as its own evidence (`role` `amendment`), which the conduct names in `amendmentEvidenceIds`; a law change lists the amendments that concern it by the hash of their text, which is part of an earlier verdict's key (*Package*), so a new or changed amendment brings a change back to be judged again.
- **Requests.** The summaries' and documents' pages are read in every run while their print is open, and once for a closed print (*Chamber data rules*, Closed prints), the turns of the stenographic record in every run, their files through the document cache (*Chamber data rules*, Document files), all on the one psp.cz schedule: in the run, 37 summary pages, 511 document pages, and 154 turns, besides the six open-data files.
- **Quality.** Every run reports the summaries read and without a file, the amendments listed and the committee amendments among them, the documents listed, not in `sd.zip`, and without a readable text, the amendments used, the written amendments to a bill with a summary that it does not list, the proposer names matched, matched despite a misspelt surname, and not matched, the votes that can be on an amendment, those linked, those not linked by reason, and the turns read.

## Collection of Laws data rules

These rules were verified against the e-Sbírka REST API on 6 October 2026 by collecting from 15 December 2025: all 765 acts of 2025 and 2026 (1 to 586 of 2025, 1 to 179 of 2026) were read, of which 151 promulgated since that day were government regulations (51) or decrees (100), and 104 of them were used.

- **Which acts.** The Collection numbers its acts of each year from 1 without gaps, so every act promulgated since the source's `bootstrapFrom` is found by asking for each number of each year from that year on (`/dokumenty-sbirky/{act}`), until three numbers in a row are not held. An act's short citation (`zkracenaCitace`) names its kind: one starting “Nařízení vlády č.” is a **government regulation**, one starting “Vyhláška č.” a **decree**; every other act (an act of Parliament, a notice, a Constitutional Court judgment) is not used. What an act is never changes, so its citation, title, and promulgation day are kept in the permanent per-act cache (*Law-change context*, *Cache*) and asked for again only when e-Sbírka reports the act changed.
- **Who and when.** The wording as promulgated (`0000-00-00`) is read whole (*Law-change context*). Its heading states the adoption date (“ze dne 23. září 2026”), and a sentence names who issues the act and under which act: “Vláda nařizuje podle … zákona č. 262/2006 Sb. …” or “Ministerstvo vnitra stanoví podle § 29 odst. 2 zákona č. 266/2025 Sb. …”, in the heading or, in a decree of several parts, at the start of each part. An issuing sentence ends with a colon: in the check all 152 did, and none of the 5 sentences of provisions or annexes that also say someone “stanoví podle” something. A regulation is the government's; a decree is a ministry's when its issuer starts with “Ministerstvo”, also when it acts with another body (“Ministerstvo vnitra v dohodě s Ministerstvem financí”, “Ministerstvo zemědělství a Ministerstvo životního prostředí”). A decree of another authority (in the check: 9 of the State Office for Nuclear Safety, 5 of the Czech National Bank, 2 each of the Energy Regulatory Office, the Czech Telecommunication Office, and the Digital and Information Agency) is not conduct of any party and is only counted. The adoption date, not the promulgation day, decides which government adopted an act: 26 acts promulgated on or after 15 December 2025 were adopted by the previous government. Zero-width characters in the wording (a zero-width space before “podle” in 582/2025 Sb.) are not white space and are removed before reading. An act whose heading states no adoption date or issuer is reported and not used.
- **Attribution.** Each act adopted by the government or a ministry on a date when a government in `automation/attribution/government-periods.json` governed is one record, its adoption, attributed to every governing party on that date ([scoring.md](scoring.md)). The record is dated by its adoption, but an act did not exist publicly before its promulgation, so a package with an earlier cutoff leaves it out.
- **Text.** The act's text is its promulgated wording without its number, its signatures, and its annexes, as in *Text from the Collection of Laws*. A newly promulgated act is first only a notice that it is being digitised (the Ministry of the Interior has 30 days for it, 222/2016 Sb., § 12(9)); until its text is there, it is reported as not yet digitised, and one still not digitised more than 30 days after its promulgation is flagged for a check in e-Sbírka. The cached notice is read again, and the act used, once e-Sbírka's list of changed wordings names its promulgated wording (*Cache*): the list names that wording, by the promulgation day, again weeks after promulgation (on 6 October 2026, for example, 151 to 157/2026, promulgated from 26 to 31 August 2026, were named as changed after 17 September 2026). In the check, 1 of the 151 acts was not yet digitised, 6 days after its promulgation.
- **One law change.** A regulation or decree is one law change with one version, `adopted`: its whole text as adopted. It is linked to the acts it is issued under (`enablingActs`, the acts named after “podle” or “k provedení” in its issuing sentences, cited as “zákona č.”, “k zákonu č.”, or “zákona České národní rady č.”, never those of its amendment history after “ve znění”), which route it by direct links (*Route*). Its text amends no act of Parliament, so it gets no context (*Law-change context*).
- **Quality.** Every run reports the acts read, the regulations and decrees since `bootstrapFrom`, those used, those adopted before a tracked government, those of other authorities by authority, those not yet digitised, and those with an unreadable heading.

## Government resolution data rules

These rules were verified on 17 October 2026 by reading every resolution of the current government from GitHub Actions (run 39042036348): 1013 to 1047 of 2025 and 1 to 660 of 2026, 695 in all.

- **Which resolutions.** The government numbers its resolutions of each year from 1 without gaps, and a page of a number not yet used answers HTTP 500 with the portal's error page, or, since 22 October 2026, HTTP 200 with the Government Office firewall's rejection page (“Request Rejected”, about 250 bytes; on that day 1048 to 1050/2025 and 9999/2026 answered it, and 1047/2025 and 5/2026 their pages). Both count as a number not held. A rejection could also hide a page the firewall blocks, so the first rejection of every run of numbers not held is followed by the page of the last resolution held, and the run fails when that page is rejected too; each run reports in its coverage audit how many numbers were answered with the rejection page. A file of a resolution answered with the rejection page stops the run, so the resolution is never left out as unreadable. The firewall's other page, “Požadavek byl zablokován”, still stops the run at once (*Requests*). The source registry names the first resolution of the current government (`firstResolution`, 1013/2025 of 15 December 2025; 1012/2025 is of 10 December 2025, by the previous government); every run reads from it, each year's numbers from 1, until three numbers in a row are not held. A number not held between two held ones is a gap: it is reported and read again in the next run; in the check there was none.
- **Page.** A resolution's page states “Usnesení vlády č. <number> ze dne <DD. MM. YYYY>”, the adoption date; under “Soubory”, the resolution (“Usnesení”) and each annex (“Příloha č. <n>”), each as a Word file (`…/attachment/<year>/<number>/docx/` or `/doc/`, the annex `…/<n>/docx/`) and a PDF, or an annex as a spreadsheet (`/xlsx/`, `/xls/`); and its material: its title (“Název materiálu”), its reference number (“Čj. OVA”), and its eKLEP identifier (“PID”). A resolution whose page has no files and whose title is a reference number of the Government Office (“čj. V14/2026-UVCR”, “čj. D32/2026-UVCR”) is classified: it is counted and not used, and read again in every run, because it can be published later (20 in 2026 in the check). The material of 26 published resolutions has no PID, for example an appointment.
- **Text.** The text of a resolution is the Word file of the resolution, then each annex's, in the order of the page, each annex after a line with its label: a resolution often approves a document “uvedený v příloze”. Each Word file is read like a print text (*Chamber data rules*, Print text): DOCX or DOC, by its link, which in the check matched every one of the 833 files' content (788 DOCX, 45 DOC). An annex without a Word file is read from its XLSX workbook, when it has one: each sheet in order (named when there are several), each non-empty row as its cells joined by “ | ”, an empty cell kept as an empty position so every value stays under its column header, a cell as the workbook stores it (a formula as its last computed value, a number without binary floating-point noise, a number in a date or time format as its date, time, or both); in the check, this gave the same cells in the same positions as an independent reader (openpyxl 3.1.5) for all 29 such workbooks, 623 of whose rows have empty cells between filled ones: lists of state property transferred, of flights of constitutional officials, of the National Recovery Plan's spending, and of gifts for war graves. A PDF and an XLS file are never read: a resolution with an annex only in those formats, or listed without any file, is held back, because what it approves may be in that annex (in the check, 50/2026, an XLS and a PDF of a programme's binding indicators, and 179/2026, a PDF of a committee's statute). In the check, every one of the 675 published resolutions had a Word file; they had 189 annexes, 158 with a Word file, 29 only as XLSX, 1 as XLS and PDF, and 1 only as PDF; a resolution's own text had a median of 935 characters, an annex 2,703, the longest 197,162. A resolution that is not classified and has no Word file of its own is reported and not used; one with a file that cannot be read as text is reported, not used, and read again in the next run, so it is not cached.
- **Duplicates of other conduct.** Many resolutions only approve what another row of the conduct table already records: a government bill or treaty, which the Chamber records as its print; a government opinion on another proposer's bill; or a government regulation, which the Collection of Laws records. Such a resolution is left out, so the same conduct is never assessed twice. It is found by its material's PID: a PID that the Chamber links to a print of kind 1, 3, or 4 (`tisk_eklep.unl`, with `cislo_za` 0), or to a sub-print of kind 7, a government opinion (`tz_eklep.unl`); or a PID that the eKLEP open data of any year from the source's `bootstrapFrom` to the current one (`requests[].pid`; in the check, the file of 2024 named the material of none of the 695 resolutions) names with the type (`requests[].type.id`) `NNV` (draft government regulation), `NZ` (draft bill, which becomes a government proposal), or `PNZ`, `SNZ`, or `NKZ` (a bill of members, the Senate, or a regional assembly, on which the government gives its opinion). A resolution whose material the Chamber links to a print of another kind (5 or 53: a state fund's budget, a deployment of the armed forces) is not left out, because those prints are not conduct. In the check, 188 resolutions were left out (50 on a government bill, 12 on a treaty, 78 on a government opinion, 11 of them neutral, 44 on a regulation, 3 on a bill and 1 on a member bill not yet linked by the Chamber), and each of them approved its material or adopted the opinion (“schvaluje” 96, “přijímá” 79, “souhlasí” 12), except the one that took note of the draft state budget for 2027 (“bere na vědomí”), which the budget print records; 487 remained, of which 2 are held back for an annex that is not read (*Text*), so 485 are used.
- **Requests.** All requests to the Government Office (`odok.gov.cz` and `www.odok.gov.cz`) take turns on one schedule: one at a time and at least 6 s apart (at most 10 a minute), with the user agent `Slibomer/1.0 (+https://slibomer.cz)`. Its firewall answers a client that does not say who it is (the user agent of curl, or a bare `Mozilla/5.0`) with HTTP 200 and a page of 323 bytes titled “Požadavek byl zablokován”; such an answer stops the run at once, without a retry. A failed connection, an HTTP 429, 502, 503, or 504, and a request with no answer within 60 s are retried up to 10 times, after 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 s and then 32 s each, never sooner than 6 s after the last request; any other HTTP error fails the run. In the check, 31 of 1,567 requests lost their connection, and each succeeded at its second or third attempt; at 20 requests a minute from another address, a connection was lost every 3 to 10 requests. Every request (time, URL, attempt, status, bytes, duration, error) is appended to `government-resolutions-requests.jsonl` in the run artifact.
- **Cache.** A published resolution does not change, so each one read is written at once to the audit state, one append-only file per year (`automation/government-resolutions/<year>.jsonl`): its number, date, title, reference number, PID, and each file's label, link, SHA-256, and extracted paragraphs, with the time it was read. It is never requested again; only a classified resolution, a resolution published without a Word file of its own, with a file that cannot be read, or with an annex in no format that is read (it is held back until a readable file appears), a number not yet held, and the eKLEP open data, which change, are read in every run. The current year's eKLEP file can be missing early in the year (`requests_2027.json` answered HTTP 404 on 18 October 2026); the run then reports it and holds back the resolutions adopted that year, because whether they only approve a draft regulation or bill is not yet known, until a run reads the file. A failed run keeps what it read. Reading every resolution from an empty cache took 157 minutes in the check (before the 29 XLSX annexes were read too), so the collection step stops reading new resolutions after 120 minutes and fails the run before any paid step, within the step's own limit of 210 minutes, which also leaves room for the Chamber's amendments; the next run continues from the cache.
- **Attribution.** A resolution is dated by its adoption date and is conduct of every governing party on that day ([scoring.md](scoring.md)); the page is public when the resolution is read. A resolution adopted before a tracked government governed is only counted.
- **Law changes.** A resolution is one law change with one version, `adopted`: its text as adopted. A resolution on the government's plan of legislative work (its title names a “plán legislativních prací vlády”) is divided instead, so that a promise is matched with the task that concerns it and not with the whole plan: 175/2026, about 149,000 tokens whole, was in 501 of the packages of 24 October 2026. Its law changes are the resolution itself; each task of a table of its annexes (a table starts with its heading, a ministry or a period, and its header row “č.”, “předkladatel”, …; a task is a row that starts with its number and a submitter's one-word abbreviation, “1”, “PV”), with its table's heading and header row and its entries in the tables that relate the tasks to EU law and to the government's programme priorities (“Vztah výše uvedených úkolů k …”, by the task's number); and, per annex, the rest of its text (its title, contents, abbreviations, explanations, the headings and header rows of its tables, any text after a table's last row, which ends with its last flag “ano” or “ne”, and the numbers of tasks read once). A task listed again in the same annex in the same words (the plan lists its tasks by ministry and again by month) is read once, and what the later listing relates it to joins it; one that differs, even in a letter's case, is a law change of its own. A table whose rows are not numbered 1, 2, 3, … leaves the resolution one law change. An annex without such a table, such as the overviews of implementing EU law, is one law change. So divided, 175/2026 is 193 law changes of at most about 12,000 tokens. A resolution amends no act, so it gets no context, and no act is linked to it, so it is routed only by tags (*Route*).
- **Quality.** Every run reports, in its log and the coverage audit, the resolutions read from the cache and from the portal, those classified, those left out as duplicates by kind, those without a readable text, those held back (for their year's eKLEP data or an annex that is not read), those adopted before a tracked government, those used, and the requests made and retried.

## Law-change context

An amending text often does not state the wording it changes ("V § 52 odst. 1 se věta poslední zrušuje."). Every model that reads a law change therefore also receives its **context**: the wording of each provision its amending instructions name, as in force when the party acted. Context comes from the Collection of Laws, not from the party or the Chamber; it is not evidence of conduct, is not an evidence item, and never changes attribution or what counts as conduct.

**Source.** The public REST API of e-Sbírka, the official electronic Collection of Laws, `https://api.e-sbirka.gov.cz`, as its official description (`https://e-sbirka.gov.cz/restful-api`, with the OpenAPI definition and user manual it links) defines it. The API needs a registered key, sent in the `esel-api-access-key` header; the workflow reads it from the repository secret `ESEL_API_ACCESS_KEY`. **The project's key may send at most 5 requests per second, as agreed with the operator; a run sends at most 4, the most [decisions.md](decisions.md) allows, to keep a margin** (*Requests*). The web application's internal interface and the SPARQL open data are never used. Checked against the API on 30 September 2026: the list of wordings of 6 acts (up to 153 wordings each) and the promulgation day of 10 acts matched the SPARQL open data; the amendments named for 17 of 20 wordings matched their consolidation links there, and for the other 3 (the newest wordings) only the API named them; the promulgated 175/2025 Sb. holds § 13 to 27, with the same text as its wording from 1 July 2026, when they took effect.

**Rules.**

- **Instructions.** Each version of a law change that amends an act is read instruction by instruction, only outside quoted new text. The introduction of the act (`Zákon č. … se mění takto:`) and renumbering (`Dosavadní odstavce … se označují jako …`) are not instructions. An instruction names its targets in the act it amends, or in the act it names: a § (`V § 52 …`, `§ 7 zní:`) and a paragraph and letter it changes in place (`odst. 1`, `odstavec 2`, `písm. a)`; when it inserts a new paragraph, letter, or point, the whole §); an article and its point, also of an amending act (`V čl. II bodu 5 zákona č. 182/2024 Sb.`); an annex (`V příloze č. 3`, the only annex `Příloha k zákonu`) or an item or point of it (`V položce 18`, `bod 2`); a footnote (`Poznámka pod čarou č. 79`); and, for a newly inserted §, the § after which it is inserted. A range of §§ or footnotes (`§ 5 až 7`, `§ 5a až 5c`) is one target that holds every § or footnote of the wording numbered within it, including those with letters (`§ 5a` lies between `§ 5` and `§ 6`), which the text does not name; a range of paragraphs is listed, as paragraphs carry no letters. An instruction with a list of paragraphs that cannot be read in full names no target rather than only some.
- **Wording.** The wording used is the one in force on the date of the earliest conduct that concerns the version (its submission or vote), with `datumUcinnostiZneniOd` on or before that date and `datumUcinnostiZneniDo`, if any, on or after it. A future wording whose effect the act states only in words has no date of effect (e-Sbírka numbers it in the year 3000) and is never used. A provision missing from it is taken from elsewhere only as *Promulgation of a later wording* says. A version without conduct gets no context.
- **Locating a provision.** A provision is found only by its ELI path in that wording (`…/par_52`, `…/par_52/odst_1`, `…/cl_2/bod_5`, `…/prilohy/priloha_3`, `…/pol_18`, `…/poznamkypodcarou/ppc_79`; the only, unnumbered annex is `priloha_0`), never by its citation label, which some nodes lack. A wording is read whole, once: the API returns the nodes of a wording (their ELI paths and texts, in document order) only in pages of 1,000, and the structure of the whole wording is needed to tell a missing provision from a present one. The text of a target is that of its node and its descendants, in document order. A provision inside another supplied provision of the same wording is supplied once, within it. The models read, after each version's text, the named provisions with their statuses and the text of each supplied provision; a provision with the same text as for an earlier version of the same law change is referred to, not repeated, and the instructions, which are in the version's text, are not repeated.
- **Statuses.** Every target gets exactly one status, which the models see with the context:

| Status | Meaning | Context supplied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `found` | The provision exists in the wording on the conduct date. | Its text, with that wording's date. |
| `later-wording` | It is missing on that date but exists in a later wording made only by amendments already promulgated on that date, with a later effect. | Its text from the nearest later wording, marked with that wording's date. |
| `promulgated-wording` | It is missing on that date, a later wording that holds it cannot be shown to be promulgated by then, and the act as it was itself promulgated on or before that date holds it (a part of the act with a later effect). | Its text as promulgated, marked with the promulgation day; amendments made since are not in it. |
| `unverified-later-wording` | It is missing on that date, not in the act as promulgated, and only in a later wording whose amendments e-Sbírka does not name or date; none of them is known to be promulgated after the conduct. | Its text from the nearest such later wording, marked as not verified to have been known on that date. |
| `paragraph-not-found` | The § or article exists, the named paragraph, letter, or point does not. | The enclosing provision (the paragraph for a missing letter, otherwise the whole § or article). |
| `new-provision` | The instruction itself inserts the provision. | None; there is no earlier text. |
| `not-found` | No wording above contains it, or the nearest later wording that does was made by an amendment promulgated after the conduct; also a missing item or point of an annex, whose whole annex (for example a long fee schedule) is never supplied in its place. | None. |
| `not-recognised` | The instruction names no target the rules above recognise (for example a whole part, head, or division, a title, or a replacement of words in the whole act). | None; the instruction is quoted. |

  When the § or article is taken from a later or the promulgated wording and the named paragraph, letter, or point is not in it, the status says where the text is from, and the models read that the whole provision is supplied.

  A law change that amends no act (a new act, transitional or repealing provisions, a treaty, a budget) gets no context and no status.
- **Promulgation of a later wording.** A later wording is used only when every amendment that took effect in it, and in every wording between the conduct date and it, was promulgated on or before the conduct date; otherwise its text could not have been known when the party acted. The amendments of a wording are the acts that the history of the act (`/dokumenty-sbirky/{act}/historie`) names for it (`novely`); an act was promulgated on the day of its `datumCasVyhlaseni`, which is the promulgation day, not the day of effect (89/2012 Sb.: 22 March 2012). A wording for which e-Sbírka names no amendment, or not the promulgation day of one, such as one where a later part of the act itself takes effect, cannot be shown to be promulgated in time. When the nearest later wording that holds the provision is such a wording, and no wording on the way to it was made by an amendment known to be promulgated after the conduct, the provision is taken from the act as it was itself promulgated (the wording `0000-00-00`), when that was on or before the conduct date and holds it, because that text was public then; otherwise from that later wording, marked as not verified, because some text marked so serves the models better than none. A provision in no such later wording, such as one repealed before the conduct, is never taken from the act as promulgated.
- **Requests.** A run sends only these requests, one at a time: once per run (a check records the workflow run and attempt, and a later step of the same run uses it), before anything else is read, the wordings e-Sbírka changed and those it withdrew since the previous check (`/dokumenty-sbirky/zmeny-zneni` and `/dokumenty-sbirky/depublikovana-zneni`, `datumCasOdberuOd` the time of the previous check less one hour, `start=0&pocet=10000`, the most the API returns for one request); then, only for what the cache lacks or holds out of date, the history of one act (its wordings with their dates and amendments, and the day it was promulgated), the nodes of one wording, page by page from page 0 (`/dokumenty-sbirky/{wording}/fragmenty?cisloStranky=`), and the detail of one act, for the day an amending act, or an act whose text as promulgated is read, was promulgated. **Requests start at least 250 ms apart, also across steps (each step's first request waits 250 ms after its client starts), so a run never sends more than 4 a second, the most [decisions.md](decisions.md) allows, below the 5 a second agreed with the operator.** The act or wording is one path segment with its slashes encoded (`%2Fsb%2F2025%2F175%2F2026-07-01`). An answer that the document does not exist (HTTP 400 `DOKUMENT_NENALEZEN`) is complete and only sets the status. A node without an ELI path and without text (a block that wraps repealing or effect provisions, whose content follows in nodes that have both) is skipped. A wording must come whole: every node with text has an ELI path, no node is listed twice, every page is of the wording asked for (a wording by date returns the wording in force on that date, which may be another), all pages reporting the same number of pages, every page but the last full, and some node with text; anything else, and a wording listed in the history that the API does not return, is a technical failure, because caching it would lose the missing text for good. A request also fails technically when it gets no answer within 60 s, an HTTP error (including a rejected key or the rate limit), or an unreadable answer. A failed request is retried up to 10 times, after 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 s and then 32 s each (about 3 minutes in all); after the last retry a technical failure fails the run. The run can simply be started again: an answer is cached only once it is complete, a failed run keeps the cache, and a check of changes, once recorded, keeps what it named out of date until it is read again. A rejected key (HTTP 401 or 403) is not retried and fails the run at once. Waiting never shortens the interval between requests below 250 ms. Every request (time, purpose, path, duration, outcome, bytes) is written to the run artifact, by the step that sent it: `print-parts/e-sbirka-requests.jsonl` (an act as promulgated, *Text from the Collection of Laws*) and `law-context/e-sbirka-requests.jsonl` (context).
- **Cache.** Every node of a wording (path and text, in document order), the list of an act's wordings, and the promulgation day, short citation, and title of an act are stored in the audit state, one append-only file per act (`automation/law-context/<year>-<number>.jsonl`), each line with the time it was read (`readAt`, UTC); a later line replaces an earlier one. The date of effect does not show that a wording stays the same: a new amendment adds a wording, shortens the one before it, and can change a future wording (checked on 30 September 2026: 155/2026 Sb. added the wording of 262/2006 Sb. from 29 August 2026 and ended the one from 1 January 2026 a day earlier; the wording of 89/2012 Sb. from 1 January 2027 changed). e-Sbírka lists every wording it adds, changes, or withdraws, including the new wordings of an amended act, so each check is recorded in `automation/law-context/changes.jsonl` with the wordings it names and its time. A stored answer is out of date when it was read before a check that named: for the list of an act's wordings, any wording of the act; for a wording, the promulgation day, or the citation, that wording (or the act as a whole, for a listed wording without a date of effect), where the lists name a wording by its date of effect and the promulgated wording by the day the act was promulgated (155/2026 Sb.: `2026-08-28`, checked on 30 September 2026), so a change on that day also puts the promulgated wording out of date, and while that day is unknown (e-Sbírka did not hold the act when it was asked), any listed wording of the act does; for everything, a check whose answer did not hold every change (more than 10,000). What is out of date is requested again when a run needs it; nothing else is ever requested again. The first check of a cache starts from its earliest answer, and an empty cache needs none. A run requests only what its law changes need and the cache lacks or holds out of date; only a new pipeline generation starts from an empty cache.

## Daily workflow

The workflow runs at 09:37 Europe/Prague and keeps a manual trigger. The schedule stays paused until a manual `workflow_dispatch` run of the current pipeline has been verified end to end (see `.github/workflows/collect-evidence.yml`).

1. **Collect.** Every run collects every active source completely from its `bootstrapFrom` date, so each package is built from the whole record history and a new package never loses earlier conduct. There is no manual start date or source subset. If any source fails, the run fails and nothing is saved. `checkpoints.json` only records each source's last successful collection; it never limits what is collected.
2. **Filter.** Only a record that can be attributable conduct of a tracked party under [scoring.md](scoring.md) continues: a decisive vote with a club result, an amendment presented in the second reading, a vote on one with a club majority, the submission of a member bill or government proposal, the government's agreement or disagreement with a bill it did not submit, the adoption of a government regulation or ministerial decree, or a government resolution. Attribution depends only on the record itself, so no other record can ever affect a score, and none is tagged or fetched further.
3. **Split prints into law changes.** Each print those records concern is split into law changes, each with its versions (see *Chamber data rules*); each regulation or decree is one law change with its text as adopted (see *Collection of Laws data rules*), and so is each government resolution but one on the plan of legislative work, which is divided into its tasks (see *Government resolution data rules*). A law change keeps only its versions up to the latest one that the print's conduct up to the cutoff concerns, with the acts those versions amend: a later text did not yet exist at the cutoff, and a change added only in a later text is left out. A print without a proposal text whose parts are known only from a later text is, before that text, one law change known by its title. From here on the unit is the **law change**, not the print: a law change is screened, routed, packaged, and assessed. The print only links a law change to the party's conduct on it, the votes on the print, its submission, and the government's opinion on it, each of which concerns one version.
4. **Screen and tag every law change.** Two independent screeners each read the full text of every version of every law change (a print-wide Senate resolution is not part of a law change and goes only into the package); none is skipped or dropped by rule. A law change is substantive, and is routed, when any of its versions is. Each screener returns:
   - whether the change is **substantive** or only **technical**, with a reason. A technical change changes nothing for anyone: renumbered references, a renamed authority, a legislative correction, or an adaptation to another act without new content; a government resolution that only takes note of a report on what has already happened, or approves an official trip or its costs; a resolution that appoints, removes, or entrusts anyone, or changes a statute or the organisation of a body, is substantive, also when it only takes note of it (an appointment can deliver a promise: 1043/2025 put a vice-premier at the head of the Council for Research, Development and Innovation). Any change of an amount, rate, deadline, right, obligation, the people covered, or who pays or receives is substantive, even a single number. In doubt, the change is substantive. A law change is substantive when at least one screener says so, or when a screener output is invalid;
   - the specific (`assessmentEligible`) topic tags from the taxonomy (`automation/topic-routing/taxonomy-v2.json`) it concerns, or `unknown`, with a confidence from 0 to 1.

   Each screener reads every version with its context (*Law-change context*), then the amendments to the change (*Amendment data rules*, Package).
5. **Route.** A substantive law change goes to promises in two ways, and to the promises of both. A promise holds its routing tags in `automation/topic-routing/promise-tags.json`: one or more specific tags, assigned once when the promise is catalogued and reviewed in its pull request, never by a model during a run. Statements hold no tags and are never routed, packaged, or assessed.
   - **Direct link.** An act the change amends, or the state budget print kind, is listed under a tag in `directLegalIdentifiers` of the taxonomy. The change goes to every promise with that tag.
   - **Tags.** Every specific tag that either screener gives with a confidence of at least 0.5. The change goes to every promise with that tag. An `unknown` from one screener does not block the other's tags, and a broad parent tag alone never routes a change.

   A change that reaches no promise, because it has no specific tag or no promise holds one, is reported as unrouted; that never means no progress. Models may return only existing tag identifiers or `unknown`. A taxonomy change is a reviewed change, and its content hash recomputes every dependent classification. A promise and a routed law change continue only when the promise's party has attributable conduct on the change's print; routing never establishes attribution or a score.
6. **Check relevance.** For each remaining promise and law change, two independent checks read the promise (its title, which names the commitment assessed, and its exact wording) and the full text of every version of the change with its context and the amendments to it and say whether the change is relevant to that promise, with a reason. The pair is kept when at least one check says relevant, or when a check output is invalid; it is dropped only when both say it is not relevant. Every dropped pair and both reasons are in the routing report. Once every law change is checked, each amendment the promise's package would hold (one the party's conduct on a kept law change names) is checked against the promise the same way, by two checks with their own prompt that read the amendment's whole text and the promise (`amendment-relevance-checks/` in the model cache, one journal per promise). The amendment comes first in the request, the same for every promise, so the checks of one amendment run one after the other and every later one reads it from the provider's prompt cache. An amendment both checks find not relevant leaves the package, and so does the conduct on it, its submission and every vote on it, unless the vote also names an amendment that was kept; a law change left with no conduct leaves too. Every check and both reasons are in the routing report (`amendmentRelevanceChecks`).
7. **Package.** Each promise with at least one kept law change gets one evidence package: every kept law change with the full text of each of its versions and their context, the party's attributable conduct on each of their prints (decisive votes, the submission, and the government's opinion with its text), each naming the version it concerns, once per print, the Senate resolution when a vote in the package followed the Senate's return, and, once each, the text of every amendment its conduct names. A law change carries only the versions up to the latest one its conduct concerns: a later text adds nothing to judging that conduct and may not have existed at the package cutoff. No version, text, or conduct of a law change in the package is ever left out, and no excerpt is selected. A law change is left out of a promise's package only when its latest verdict in an earlier assessment of that promise (the most recently evaluated proposal in the review manifest that judged it) has both assessor outputs valid and both saying not relevant, and what that verdict judged is unchanged: the law change's versions with their text and context as in that package, the texts of the amendments that concern it, the promise's title and wording, and the assessor prompt with the scoring rule (one hash, `irrelevance-key-v1`). A new vote on the same text keeps it out, because relevance depends on what the law change says; a new version, another context, another promise wording, or another assessor prompt brings it back. A law change whose context was trimmed in the assessed package (`generation.contextOmissions`) gives no verdict. The conduct that concerns only left-out changes, and a Senate resolution no remaining change names, leave with them. The package lists each left-out change in `excludedLawChanges` (evidence ID, title, the proposal, both reasons), which the assessors read and never cite, the proposal page shows, and the routing report counts (`excludedIrrelevantPairCount`, with the list). A promise all of whose law changes with conduct are left out gets no package. In run 73's data with the prompt then active, this left out 1,738 promise and law-change pairs: 541 packages were 27 % smaller, and 147 promises got none. Every package is assessed, whatever its size, as long as the models can read it: their context window is 1,050,000 tokens, their answer included (OpenAI model documentation, verified on 26 September 2026). Every answer is capped at 48,000 tokens, its reasoning included (the longest of the 1,456 assessor answers of runs 72 to 75 took 20,194), so a request can carry at most 1,002,000 input tokens. An answer cut off at the cap is an invalid output, handled like any other invalid output of its step. The arbiter reads the package with both reviews, so 32,000 of these tokens are kept for the reviews (both reviews of run 75 took at most 11,840 tokens, for 38 law changes; its largest packages have 58), and a package may take at most 970,000, or 1,002,000 when even without any context it takes more. When the assessor request, the instructions and the package together (estimated at 2.5 characters per token, below the fewest measured in run 56), is larger, only **context** is left out, never a law change's text: the whole context of the law change with the most context is left out, then of the next, until the package fits; then the provisions left out are put back, shortest first, as many as still fit, so that the most provisions keep their wording; every size is measured on the whole package as it is sent, including the record of what was left out. A provision with the same text in several versions of one law change is left out or put back in all of them. Each version's context names the provisions whose wording it does not supply and says why, and the package records them per law-change version (`generation.contextOmissions`: per act and wording, the node paths, from which the citations follow). In run 75's data, 9 of 706 packages needed this (13 before the answer cap), every one of them holding print 67 (a large amendment of the Building Act); summaries of the context written by a model were tried instead and rejected, because a stronger model found 52 % of 1,342 summarised provisions unfaithful. A package that is still too large without any context cannot be sent and is reported for human review. The arbiter's request is still checked before it is called; when it is larger than 1,002,000, the arbiter is not called and the disagreement goes to human review (in run 75, before the reserve and the cap, one fitted package's arbiter request came to about 931,000, above the 922,000 then allowed). A request above 272,000 input tokens costs twice as much for input and one and a half times as much for output, and the spend report counts it so (`automation/model-prices.json`).
8. **Persist.** Before any assessment call, the collection record (`checkpoints.json`) and the packages are saved together to the dedicated unpublished audit pull request, and only when collection, splitting, context, screening, routing, relevance checks, packaging, and persistence all succeed in the same run. The model caches and the law-change context cache are saved to the same pull request after every run, a failed one too, before the collection record and packages: each of their entries is written only once it is complete, so what a failed run paid for or read is kept (*Resuming*). The queue keeps at most one current package per promise: a rebuilt package whose assessable content is unchanged keeps the current one, a promise that no longer gets a package has its current package withdrawn, and superseded and withdrawn versions stay auditable.
9. **Assess.** Each new or changed package, up to the per-run cap, goes independently to two assessors, who may cite only evidence IDs in that package. Each assessor first gives a verdict on **every** law change in the package: relevant or not relevant to the exact promise, with a reason. Each conduct is then judged against the version it concerns, read with its context ([scoring.md](scoring.md)). For each relevant law change, the assessor also states its effect (`effectCz`): what the rule was before and what it is after, from the wording in its context, and whether it becomes stricter or looser; the conduct is judged by that effect, not by the change's title or stated purpose. An output that omits a law change, gives it two verdicts, or gives a relevant change no effect is invalid. Only relevant changes may support the proposed score. An invalid output is never saved and is asked for again from the same assessor, at most twice; the invalid attempts stay in the assessment's audit record. When both assessments give the same score, no arbiter is called: when both find the evidence sufficient, the assessments agree; when only one does, that one carries the candidate; when neither does, a person reviews it. A package that differs from an assessed one only by leaving out the law changes both assessors of that assessment found not relevant is not assessed again: each proposal records the content hash and fingerprint of the package its own verdicts leave (`reducedAssessmentContentHash`, `reducedAssessmentFingerprint`), and a package with that fingerprint keeps the proposal. Rebuilt from run 73's data, every one of the 541 smaller packages had exactly that fingerprint. A package that was fitted by leaving out context can, once smaller, fit more of it; it then differs from what was assessed and is assessed again. Only when the valid assessments give different scores, or exactly one output is still invalid after its retries, does the arbiter, which reads the whole package and both assessments, select a usable assessment or return no conclusion (in run 62, before this rule, assessments with the same score made 59 of the 165 arbiter calls and 49% of their cost). An arbiter request estimated above 272,000 input tokens, which is billed at the long-context price, goes to the assessors' model instead of the arbiter model, with the highest reasoning effort it supports (`max`), so that it reasons more than the assessors whose disagreement it decides (in run 72 such requests were 19 of 69 arbiter calls and 90% of their cost; in run 78 the 24 such arbiter answers took a median of 1,761 reasoning tokens at the default effort, and their output cost about 0.02 USD of their 1.58 USD). That model and its effort are not part of the fingerprint, so the change reassesses no package, and the assessment's audit record names the model that answered and its reasoning effort. Two outputs still invalid after their retries write a private failure audit and re-enter a later batch unless a newer package replaces them. The cap (`max_assessments`, default `0`) is set on each manual run; packages above it stay queued for a later run, and the run reports their number and size so the cost can be estimated first. A scheduled run assesses nothing until a cap is set for it.
10. **Report.** The proposals and a coverage audit are written to the audit pull request. The audit covers every promise and active source and reports the run's data-quality figures: records skipped as not attributable, prints with a proposal, approved, or passed text, prints with a text from the Collection of Laws, government opinions that are conduct and those without a readable text, the amendments' figures (*Amendment data rules*, Quality), the government resolutions' figures (*Government resolution data rules*, Quality), law changes added or removed during the debate, approved bills without an approved text, law changes, technical and substantive changes, direct links, unrouted law changes, pairs dropped by the relevance check, pairs left out because both assessors of an earlier assessment found them not relevant, packages larger than the models can read, packages that fit only without part of their context, invalid model outputs, and, for the context, the law changes that amend an act, the targets by status, and the e-Sbírka requests made and failed. The pull request text lists only what needs a person: every current proposal whose status differs from the promise's current public score (the latest revision in `data/assessment-revisions` on `main`), with its party, both statuses, how the proposal arose, and each assessor's and the arbiter's verdict with its reason; every current proposal left for human review; and every package larger than the models can read. Each proposal records the public revision it was compared with. It leaves the list when a later public revision is published, whatever score that revision gives, or when a newer proposal supersedes it. A listed proposal is marked when this run created it, when its package has changed since and no new proposal has been made yet, when the promise no longer has a current package, or when its package fit the models only without part of its context; the proposal page then lists, per law change, the provisions whose wording the assessors did not have, each linked to that wording in e-Sbírka. Every proposal page shows, per law change, each assessor's relevance verdict with its reason and, for a relevant change, the effect it stated, the law changes left out because an earlier assessment found them not relevant, with both reasons, and the law changes its own assessors both found not relevant, which later packages leave out while the proposal stays current for them. New proposals that confirm the public score, unchanged packages, packages deferred by the cap, and failed assessments are only counted; the full list is in the run's `assessment-batch.json`. Model-written text is shown as plain text. When the list does not fit GitHub's size limit for the pull request text, it continues, in order and complete, in numbered comments on the pull request; each run edits its previous continuation comments in place, adds missing ones, and deletes extra ones, so they never pile up. A reason longer than 1,500 characters is cut in the list and kept whole on the proposal page, and at most 40 continuation comments are written; anything beyond them is counted and left to the proposal pages and `assessment-batch.json`. The text also counts the law changes the assessors found not relevant, by the tag or direct link that routed them, so an overly broad tag is visible.

**Run log.** Every step writes what it is doing to the GitHub Actions log: which source it downloads, how many items it processes, and progress in batches. Every paid model call writes one line with its model, processing tier, tokens, and estimated cost, and is appended to `model-calls.jsonl` in the run artifact. Every download attempt to psp.cz for print texts (its time, duration, HTTP status, or the transport error code) and every new connection opened to psp.cz, or to the proxy when `PSP_PROXY_URL` is set (how long it took to connect, or why it failed) are appended to `print-parts/psp-requests.jsonl` in the run artifact as they happen, so the file survives a failed download and a step that times out, and the log totals them with the most attempts and connections started within any 60 seconds, so the site's limits can be measured. The run summary totals the estimated spend by step and model, even after a failure. Prices are in `automation/model-prices.json` with their official source and verification date; input written to the provider's prompt cache is priced at its cache-write rate, which is higher than the uncached input rate, and cached input at the cached rate. The cost is an estimate, not the provider invoice. The run artifact (`evidence-pipeline-<run ID>`, the files under `automation/runs/pipeline/github-actions/`) is kept for 7 days: the account's Actions storage holds 500 MB, an artifact took about 22 MB in run 83 (22 October 2026), and what later runs and reviews need is on the audit branch (caches, packages, proposals).

**Resuming.** A run can stop at any point: exhausted API credit (not retried, *Audit and cost control*), a download error, or a timeout. Every paid result is saved as soon as it exists: each screened law change and relevance check goes to its model cache, each valid assessor or arbiter response of an unfinished assessment goes to `automation/review-proposals/responses/` until that assessment is saved (an invalid one is never reused, so it is asked for again; it is written beside them at once, so a resumed run keeps it in the audit record and counts it against the retries), and each finished assessment goes to its proposal and the proposal manifest. When the assessment stops part-way, the run saves every finished proposal, reports what was not assessed, and still fails. Running the workflow again repeats only the free work (collection, print texts, packaging) and pays only for what is still missing. At most the calls in flight when the run stopped are lost. The job may run for 360 minutes, GitHub's longest. Each paid step (screening and relevance checks, then assessment) may use what is left of it when the step starts, a started minute counting as spent, less 15 minutes for the steps that save the paid work (in run 80, 20 October 2026, which assessed nothing, the steps after the relevance checks took under a minute), so it ends before the job limit. With fewer than 5 minutes left, the run stops before the step, and the next run continues. The steps before them have fixed limits, so a run in which they run long can still reach the job limit before a paid step starts. The relevance checks answered about 70 promise and law-change pairs a minute in run 80.

The audit pull request is never merged to `main`, and its Git history is not an audit record. The workflow never changes public data, a public score, or publication status. A failed complete run does not advance public freshness.

## Pipeline generation

`automation/pipeline-generation` names the current generation of the audit state. The workflow restores the collection record, caches, and queues from the audit branch only when that branch holds the same generation. When a rule change makes saved audit state invalid, change the generation: the next run starts from empty caches and queues, so everything is screened, checked, and assessed again. Old audit state is never repaired or reused.

## From proposal to public score

Human review may establish that a score should change. That change is a separate scoped pull request based on current `main`, which adds an append-only assessment revision citing official sources and follows [release-process.md](release-process.md).

The revision is created from the reviewed proposal with `node scripts/create-assessment-revision.mjs --proposal <proposal directory> --date <YYYY-MM-DD>`. It refuses a proposal that is not a candidate, that the review manifest marks as superseded, whose evidence package or assessment run does not match the one it records, or that was compared with an older public revision than the current one, and a date before the current revision or the package. It checks the revision against the public schema before writing it. It copies the candidate status, the proposed public explanation, and the evidence trail: the law changes the chosen assessment found relevant and cited, or on which it cited conduct, grouped by print, with the cited conduct on them and the cited Senate resolutions, each with its official link, and, when the package fit only without part of its context, the provisions whose wording the assessment did not have, each linked to that wording in e-Sbírka ([data-model.md](data-model.md)). The reviewer may correct the explanation in the pull request; the trail is published exactly as copied, so the public page shows only what the reviewed proposal cited.

## Audit and cost control

A run makes only the requests and model calls its work needs, and repeats none whose result cannot have changed ([decisions.md](decisions.md)). Each AI request has its own prompt in `automation/prompts/`, written only for that request. The assessors and the arbiter receive their prompt followed by [scoring.md](scoring.md) unchanged, so no prompt restates a scoring rule. The assessors' and arbiter's active prompt versions, providers, model IDs, confidence, evidence IDs, and input fingerprint remain auditable. The fingerprint covers assessable content with its context, active prompts with the scoring rule, providers, and models (the long-context arbiter model aside, *Assess*), so an unchanged package is never assessed again. The screening, relevance, assessor, and arbiter models are set in the workflow and recorded in every run. Each model cache (`automation/topic-routing/tag-cache/`, append-only journals with one line per result; the relevance checks in one file per promise, `relevance-checks/<party-period>/<promise>.jsonl`, so no file nears GitHub's file-size limit) records every accepted result's input hash (of its input blocks as sent: the text with its context, and the taxonomy or the promise), taxonomy hash where it applies, prompt hash, provider, and models, so an unchanged law change or pair is never sent to a model again. A new screening or relevance-check prompt keeps the results of the prompt before it, so a better prompt does not pay for every result again: its declaration in `scripts/lib/law-change-screening.mjs` names the content hash of the prompt before and the kinds of law change whose results are not kept (`rerun`: `chamber-print` for every other Chamber print, `treaty`, `government-resolution`, `government-regulation`, `ministerial-decree`), and only those, and the law changes and pairs whose input changed, are sent again. A result is kept only with both answers, only while its input, taxonomy, provider, and models are the same, and only while the current output rules accept it; the run that first reuses it appends one line naming the prompt that kept it (`keptUnderPromptContentHash`) beside the hash of the prompt that made it, so a later prompt can keep it again and each result still names its prompt. The run report gives each result's prompt when it is not the current one and counts those results. A run fails at once when a prompt keeps the results of a prompt under which no complete cached pair was made or kept while the cache holds results and none under the current prompt, because then its hash is wrong and it would send everything again. A prompt that keeps nothing (`null`) sends everything again. The assessors' and arbiter's results are reused only under the same prompts. A manual run can set a spend limit (`max_spend_usd`): before each paid call it adds up the estimated cost of the run's calls so far, those of its earlier steps included (`model-calls.jsonl`), and once that reaches the limit it makes no further call and fails, keeping every result it paid for (the model caches are saved also when a run fails); the calls already sent still finish and are paid, so the spend can pass the limit by what they cost.

Every model call uses OpenAI Flex processing: the same models at half the standard price, answered more slowly (OpenAI pricing and Flex documentation, verified on 27 September 2026). A request waits up to 30 minutes for its answer; a Flex timeout (408), a rate limit or unavailable Flex capacity (429, not charged), a provider error (5xx), a request that fails without a response, and a successful response whose body cannot be read are retried with backoff, up to eight attempts, for about four minutes without a Retry-After header; a Retry-After header can lengthen a wait but never shorten it (with Retry-After 300, a request can wait about 35 minutes), and each wait is jittered, so parallel requests at a token limit do not all retry at once. Each retry of a refused request (an error status) is logged with the provider's reason (the error's type, code, and the start of its message, with account identifiers and key fragments left out) and its Retry-After header, and a request refused for good fails with the same reason and header, so the log tells a rate limit from unavailable Flex capacity or an exhausted quota. A refusal for billing, which the provider answers with 429 and `error.type` `insufficient_quota` or one of the codes `credit_balance_exhausted`, `organization_spend_limit_exceeded`, `project_spend_limit_exceeded`, and `organization_usage_limit_exceeded`, is never retried, because a retry does not restore access until credit or the limit is raised (OpenAI error-codes documentation, verified on 23 October 2026): the request fails at once and says so, its step starts no further calls and keeps every saved result, and the run fails, so a run started after the credit is added continues from what was saved (*Resuming*). A retry without a response is logged with its connection error, and a retry of an unreadable body as such. In run 83 (22 October 2026), 538 requests were refused with 429, most of them with Retry-After 300. The two calls of a pair (the two screeners, the two relevance checks, and the two assessors) are made one after the other: with the same model they send the same request, so the second reads the provider's prompt cache and pays a tenth for the input they share. Their answers stay independent, and each valid answer is saved before the next call. The provider writes and finds its prompt cache only at message ends and at breakpoints the request marks, never inside one text (OpenAI prompt-caching documentation, verified on 8 October 2026): in run 70, when a relevance check sent the law change and the promise as one text, each of its 50,706 calls read either its whole input from the cache or none of it, so the second check of a pair read it, and the first check of the next promise paid for the law change again, at the cache-write price. The screeners and the relevance checks therefore send their input as two text blocks, each marked as a cache breakpoint: the taxonomy and then the law change for a screener, the law change and then the promise for a relevance check. The relevance checks of one law change are made one after another, so after the first check its text is read from the prompt cache; different law changes are checked in parallel, the law change with the most pairs ready first. Screening and relevance checks share one pool of parallel requests: a worker screens the next law change while one is left to start, and otherwise checks the pairs of a law change already screened, so the relevance checks start as soon as no law change is left to start screening, and slow last screening calls do not hold them up. After a failure, no screening or check starts, and every finished one is kept. Checked on 8 October 2026 with a relevance check of 7,042 input tokens: a check of the same law change against another promise read 7,048 of its 7,051 input tokens from the cache, and with the change and promise sent as one text, a check against another promise read none. Re-priced so, run 70's relevance checks would have cost about 8.43 USD instead of 32.83 USD. Many law changes and packages are processed in parallel, set in the workflow, and each process keeps the input it sends to a model within 80 % of that model's usage-tier-4 limit per minute (cached input included), shared equally among the packages assessed at the same time; a single request larger than a process's share waits for that process's empty minute and is sent alone, and a rate limit that is still reached is retried as above.

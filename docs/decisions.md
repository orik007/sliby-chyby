# Current project rules

This document contains only the current confirmed rules. When a rule changes, replace this document and every implementation of the prior rule; do not keep superseded models, procedures, prompts, or decision records.

## Public identity

- The public brand is Sliboměr.
- The canonical public origin is `https://slibomer.cz`.
- Existing repository, Worker, and automation identifiers retain `sliby-chyby` until a separate infrastructure decision changes them.

## Promise catalogue

- A source is one verifiable item: one PDF, page, post, video, article, vote, or official record. It has an ID, type, title, publisher, URL, and any known date.
- A source can support many catalogue items. An item records its source ID and an exact locator, such as a PDF page.
- The catalogue covers every statement of the official national programmes of ANO 2011, SPD, and Motoristé sobě for the 2025 Czech Chamber of Deputies election in which the party says what it will do, what it wants, what it rejects, or how something will be. Every other passage of a programme is recorded with its reason as not catalogued, so that the catalogue is checked against the whole programme text. Election programmes are promise sources, never evidence of conduct.
- Every catalogued item is exactly a `promise` (slib), a `statement` (výrok), or `rejected` (zamítnuto). A promise is an item that attributable conduct can deliver or contradict; it is public, scored, and counts in the SLAVA Index. A statement is an official item that no attributable conduct can deliver or contradict; it is public, has no score, and never counts in the index. A rejected occurrence is not public and records one technical reason code from [promise-selection.md](promise-selection.md): `duplicate`, `summary`, `list-estimate`, `not-party-statement`, or `truncated`.
- The party page shows statements only after the visitor turns on the statements filter; each statement is labelled as having no score. Every promise and statement has a public detail page with its exact quote and a link to the original programme at its page.
- A score revision made from an assessment shows its evidence trail on the promise detail page, for the current score and for every revision in its history: per Chamber print, the relevant law changes and the party's conduct on them (submission, club vote with its result, Senate return), each with a link to its official psp.cz record and the text it concerned (in e-Sbírka, for a text psp.cz does not publish). A cited government regulation or ministerial decree is shown with who adopted it and when, linked to its text in e-Sbírka. When the assessment had a law change's text without the wording of some provisions it amends, because its package would otherwise have been too large, the page says so and lists those provisions, each linked to its wording in e-Sbírka. Only links are published, never text excerpts.
- An item's quote is the exact programme wording without layout artefacts; its title names the one commitment assessed, written only from facts in the quote. The canonical rule is [promise-selection.md](promise-selection.md).
- Routing tags of a promise are assigned once when it is catalogued and reviewed in its pull request; no model tags promises.

## Site changes page

- The public page `/zmeny` lists changes to the site's features by date, newest first, and, separately, the features being prepared. It is linked from the footer.
- It lists only what a visitor can use or see on the site: pages, functions, and disclosed limitations. It never lists catalogue content, score changes, or superseded rules; score changes stay in each promise's revision history.
- Its entries are in `data/changelog.json` and validated against its schema. A pull request that adds a visitor-facing feature adds its entry; a prepared feature moves from the prepared list to a dated entry when it is released.

## Promise source archive

- Preserve the original files of every catalogued source in a versioned repository archive, independent of the automation audit branch, together with their extracted text against which every quote is checked.
- Record each copy's source ID, original URL, retrieval URL and time, byte length, and SHA-256 hash. Preserve downloaded bytes unchanged; changed source content receives a new copy rather than replacing an archived file.
- Public source links continue to point to the original publisher. An archived copy can be used as an explicitly labelled fallback if the original disappears, through the normal release process.

## Policy categories

- Every promise and statement has exactly one common policy category from the controlled 2025 category list, decided from its exact wording.
- When one item mentions several subjects, its category follows the principal commitment named by its title.
- The public site groups promises only by that common category and orders them within it by current assessment status (fulfilled, moving towards, acting against, moving against, no verified progress) and then by their order in the programme. Shown statements follow the promises of their category in programme order.
- A category is a navigational subject label, not an editorial measure of importance.

## Evidence and conduct

- Only official records are evidence. A party's own website or publication, a press release, and the media are never evidence and are not collected.
- Evidence sources are added one at a time and only after their structure is verified against official documentation and real data, their completeness is measured, and their per-run quality checks exist. The canonical source list and data rules are in [evidence-pipeline.md](evidence-pipeline.md).
- The sources are the Chamber of Deputies and, for government regulations and ministerial decrees, the Collection of Laws (e-Sbírka).
- Attributable conduct is: a decisive Chamber vote (by strict majority of the party's club), a member bill (every party with a proposing MP), a government proposal (every governing party on its date), or a government regulation or ministerial decree (every governing party on its adoption date). The canonical table is in [scoring.md](scoring.md).
- A government proposal, regulation, or decree of a ministry is conduct of all governing parties together; a decree of another authority is conduct of none. The current government of ANO 2011, SPD, and Motoristé sobě was appointed on 15 December 2025.

## Scores and index

- Every promise has one current numerical score from `-2` to `+2`. The default is `0`, except for a verified maintenance baseline defined in [scoring.md](scoring.md). A statement has no score and no score history. Score revisions are append-only while an item is a promise; a promise that becomes a statement or a rejected occurrence loses them.
- A proposal in line with or against the promise is `+1` or `-1`. A decision (a decisive vote) delivering or contradicting the whole promise is `+2` or `-2`, and only a part of it `+1` or `-1`. A decision (a decisive vote, a regulation, or a decree) outweighs a proposal; among conduct of the same weight, the most recent decides, only as far as it reaches ([scoring.md](scoring.md)). Conduct of the same weight and date, or one decision with parts for and against the promise, gives the lower score, and the explanation names both. What counts as a part against the promise, and when a generally applicable measure is conduct on a promise naming a specific project, place, or group, is defined only in [scoring.md](scoring.md). For a promise with a measurable target, conduct on that target (for a deficit target, that year's state budget) decides before conduct on its other parts.
- A score is separate from the catalogue state and may change whenever attributable official evidence justifies a new current assessment.
- The SLAVA Index of a party is derived from the current scores of all its promises, which are exactly its scored promises on the public site. It ranges from `-100` to `+100`; its formula is in [slava-index.md](slava-index.md). No manual aggregate is stored.

## Evidence automation

- **Only what the work needs, and never twice.** A run makes only the requests and model calls its own work needs; it never downloads more than it uses. What cannot change is stored in the audit state and never requested again: the result of a model call, which is reused whenever the prompt, the input with its context, the provider, and the model are the same. What e-Sbírka holds (the wordings of an act and their texts) is stored with the time it was read and requested again only when e-Sbírka's own list of changed or withdrawn wordings, checked once per run, names it since then. A source that can change and reports no changes, such as the Chamber's open data and print texts, is read again in every run, because only that shows what is new.
- The daily workflow runs at 09:37 Europe/Prague and retains a manual trigger. It is paused until a manual run of the current pipeline has been verified end to end.
- Every run collects every active source completely from its `bootstrapFrom` date, so each package is built from the whole record history. The unit of evidence is a **law change**: one part of a print's official proposal text, usually the amendment of one act, or a whole government regulation or ministerial decree, whose only version is its text as adopted. The print only links a law change to the party's attributable conduct on it (its decisive votes and its submission). A law change has versions as the bill is debated (proposed, approved by the Chamber, passed by Parliament), and each conduct is judged against the version it concerned. Print texts are downloaded again in every run. When psp.cz does not publish the text a decisive vote concerned and the bill became an act, that version's text is the act as promulgated, read from e-Sbírka (its annexes left out), and the models and the public page say so; a vote that rejected the Senate's amendments concerned another text and is never judged by the act.
- psp.cz has refused every connection from GitHub Actions since 28 September 2026, so every psp.cz request of a run goes through one fixed Czech address: an HTTP proxy on a VPS rented by the owner (FORPSI, Prague), reachable only with credentials and forwarding only to `psp.cz`, configured in the repository secret `PSP_PROXY_URL`. Without the secret a run connects directly. All psp.cz requests take turns on one schedule, one at a time and at most 20 a minute.
- An amending text often does not state the wording it changes. Every model that reads a law change (screeners, relevance checks, assessors, arbiter) therefore also receives its **context**: the wording of each provision its amending instructions name, as in force on the date of the conduct on that version, from the official e-Sbírka REST API, never above its limit of 5 requests per second. Context is not evidence of conduct and never changes attribution. What is missing, and why, is stated to the models and counted in the audit. The canonical rules are in [evidence-pipeline.md](evidence-pipeline.md).
- Two independent screeners read every law change in full, with no change dropped by rule; a change is substantive when either screener says so. A substantive change is routed to promises by direct legal-identifier links or by the specific tags of either screener, to the promises holding that tag in their reviewed routing tags, and a promise and change are kept unless two independent relevance checks both find them unrelated. Statements are never routed or assessed.
- A promise's package holds every kept law change in full with the party's conduct on it, without a count limit or excerpt selection; a package is assessed whatever its size. A law change that both assessors of an earlier assessment of the promise found not relevant, both outputs valid, is left out of the promise's packages, named in one line with both reasons, while its text and context, the promise's title and wording, and the assessor prompt with the scoring rule are unchanged; a change to any of them brings it back to be judged again. A package that differs from an assessed one only by the law changes that assessment's assessors both found not relevant keeps that assessment, so leaving them out is not paid for again; only a package that was fitted by leaving out context, and now fits more of it, is assessed again, because it holds more than was assessed. When it leaves no room for the arbiter to read both reviews, context is left out, never a law change's text: the largest contexts first, then their provisions are put back, shortest first, while the package fits; a package that leaves no such room even without context is fitted only to what the models can read; what is left out is stated to the models, on the proposal page, and on the public page. Only a package still too large without any context is reported for human review. Model-written summaries of the context are not used. Each new or changed package, up to a per-run cap on paid assessments, goes to two independent assessors. An invalid assessor output is asked for again at most twice and is never saved. An arbiter is called only when the valid assessments give different scores or exactly one output is still invalid; with the same score, the assessment that finds the evidence sufficient carries the candidate, and when neither does, a person reviews it. An arbiter request above the long-context price threshold goes to the assessors' cheaper model. Each assessor states for every law change in the package whether it is relevant to the promise and why, and for a relevant one its effect: the rule before and after, from the wording in its context, and whether it becomes stricter or looser; the conduct is judged by that effect, never by the change's title or stated purpose.
- Its output is an unpublished human-review proposal on a dedicated audit pull request. It never changes public data, a public score, or publication status. A human-approved score change is a separate scoped pull request.
- Every AI request has its own prompt written only for that request, holding only what it needs. The assessors and the arbiter also receive [scoring.md](scoring.md) unchanged; no prompt restates its rules.
- The assessors and arbiter may cite only supplied evidence IDs. Their active prompt versions, providers, model IDs, confidence, evidence IDs, and input fingerprint remain auditable, and the fingerprint prevents paid reassessment of unchanged input.
- A failed complete run does not advance public freshness.
- When a rule change makes saved audit state (caches and queues) invalid, the pipeline generation changes and the next run starts from empty audit state. Old audit state is never repaired or reused.

## Delivery

- Changes for `main` use a pull request, exact-head verification, explicit owner approval (`schvaluji`), and a manual merge.
- Copilot and Codex reviews never start automatically. They run only after an explicit owner request for a specified pull request and head, and are advisory.
- Addressed review feedback receives a visible, item-by-item resolution before its corresponding line-review thread is resolved. The response protocol is defined in `release-process.md`.
- Only `main` deploys. The stack is Astro, TypeScript, plain CSS, and schema-validated versioned files.
